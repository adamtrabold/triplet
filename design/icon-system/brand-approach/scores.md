# Brand approach to icons: director scores

## Round 1: v3 cards (2026-10-14)

Judged on `concepts/sketches/v3-card-0..5.png` at phone width, starting
with card 4 (side by side). These are concept-phase scores: the idea, the
approach, and how it fits the product and the brand, judged on what the
stills actually show. A 9 means "I'd defend this to the owner as is".

**Result: not ready. Nothing goes to the owner yet.** One more round, with
the fixes below.

### Likely owner objections (to the board as a whole)

1. **"These are just generic app icons."** All three directions' tools (18
   of the ~33 icons) are a standard round-ended line set: account, filters,
   locate, ×, +, chevron. Field Guide's and Cut Paper's tools are near
   interchangeable. That's "kinda average", his word.
2. **"Why does this look like what we have?"** In rows and on the map, Field
   Guide and Cut Paper are Today plus one detail (card 4 says so honestly).
3. **"Checkmark was only supposed to be lightly rounded like the rest of
   the icons."** That's his stated corner. Timetable's mitred corners and
   flat ends contradict it, and in its own tag stub the fixed, rounded
   mark-visited check sits beside a mitred star like a foreigner.
4. **The weak drawings will pull focus:** Field Guide's street reads as a
   traffic cone, Cut Paper's as a strip of bacon, and Timetable's district
   as a first-aid box.
5. **Cut Paper:** its dotted off star reads as "missing" (the visual
   designer agrees), and its off-true lean may read as a rendering bug ("not
   following good design principles").

### What keeps blocking a 9, and why

The three directions **converge in the tools**. My own turn 3 default,
"tools carry no whimsy", turned into "tools carry no identity", so each
direction's character lives in only 11 place glyphs, and in rows those are
close to Today's. Quiet tools are right, but anonymous tools aren't. Each
direction needs a **tool grammar of its own** (terminal, corner,
construction) that you can see at 18 to 24px without adding ink. That's
the fix that applies to all three.

### Scores

**Card 0, Today: reference, not scored.** It's accurate and useful as the
baseline.

**Card 1, Field Guide: 7.**
The idea (a hierarchy by role: places are the treasure, tools are the
small print) is sound, on brand, and the calmest board. But the stills
don't show "plates". The places are Today's silhouettes plus a steam curl
or a knife, and the tools are a stock line set. As shown, it reads as
"Today, tidied".
Fixes:
1. Make the specimen register visible as **one printing device used across
   all 11 places**. The candidate I'd try first is a cream inlay *inside*
   the silhouette (two-ink print logic, like the Savoy label's cream-on-ink
   luggage pictograms). The district and street already work this way
   (cream streets, cream centre line). Make every place follow it, so a row
   badge at 18px reads as a plate, not as Today. One inlay per glyph, never
   two.
2. Give the key register its own trait: terminals and joins taken from the
   fixed visited check's softened corner (the Same Die seed). That way the
   tools are visibly kin to the stamp's check, not to Lucide.
3. Redraw street: the perspective trapezoid reads as a cone.
4. Card copy: say plainly that this is the evolutionary option, "closest to
   Today". That's a legitimate choice, and he should be able to pick it
   knowingly.

**Card 2, Cut Paper: 6.**
The promise ("the most whimsy, the most ours") isn't delivered. The lean
is barely visible at 18px and reads as error rather than hand. The tools
have become plain strokes, so "every icon is a piece cut from paper" is
false for more than half the set. The dotted star reads as missing.
It can reach 9 only if the cut is in the **silhouette**, not the angle.
One round to prove it; if the side-by-side still shows it near Field
Guide, replace it.
Fixes:
1. Drop the lean. Put the scissors in the shapes: confident curved cuts,
   flares, and bites (Matisse), clean and stable on the baseline.
2. Tools become **cut strips**: filled shapes with angle-cut ends, not
   strokes. That's the trait that separates them from Field Guide's at a
   glance. Keep them thinner than the place pieces.
3. The off star ships as the solid knife line. Drop the dotted primary, and
   move the dotted version to the dropped strip with one line.
4. Street: one cut ribbon, not a double strip.
5. Check that the account glyph in the scallop isn't timid at the strip
   weight.

**Card 3, Timetable Sorts: 8.**
It's the only direction that differs at a glance, and it holds on the map.
The proof line *is* the attitude, and the optical masters work at 12px.
It's under 9 because it breaks the owner's stated corner, the fixed check
looks foreign beside it, and its tools are still close to a stock line set
(the ink traps don't read at phone size).
Fixes:
1. Lightly rounded, per his rule: a small corner and terminal radius taken
   from the visited check, instead of mitres and flat ends. It stays a
   type-cut set through proportion, stem weight and squarish curves.
2. Re-check the tag stub: the star, the directions icon and the fixed
   mark-visited check must read as one family.
3. District: lose the cross. It reads as first aid. Use the block cut by
   streets as in the others, outline version.
4. One typographic trait in the tools you can see at 18 to 24px (the
   squarish curve on the account and locate icons, the cut proportion of
   ×/+ to the caps).
5. The card's Risks line should say "outlined icons are the common modern
   default; its character depends on the cut". That's honest, and it's his
   likely objection.

**Card 4, side by side: keep.** It's the most useful still on the board.
It's honest, and it's the one that will show whether round 2 actually
diverges. Its intro should say "this is how far apart they are" and leave
the verdict to him, so trim the editorial line.

**Card 5, dropped: keep.** Add Cut Paper's dotted star.

### Blocker added after systems' coverage check (coverage.md, jam turn 8)

**Jobs 1 and 2 are on no card.** "The brand's attitude in plain words" and
"what supports or works against it" were the owner's *first* ask ("think
of brand approach to icons conceptually — eg what is the attitude and ethos
of the brand and what visual and stylistic approaches may support or
detract from that"). Right now they live only in jam.md. That alone keeps
the board from going out, whatever the direction scores are. I should have
caught it at turn 6.

**Fix: a new card, "The brand, in plain words", in front of card 0.** One
phone-width still, short:
1. The attitude: "Printed with care, marked by hand, kept like a souvenir."
   Then "Vintage in method, not in costume." Each in one plain sentence.
2. The rule: the app prints, you stamp. Illustrate it with the real visited
   stamp (FIXED) beside a printed label.
3. Icons are the furniture and the place name is the hero, with one inspo
   crop as evidence (the Savoy luggage pictograms).
4. **Supports** and **works against**: at most 6 lines each, in plain words,
   drawn from jam turns 1–2. Supports: one drawing language, few inks, flat
   colour, lightly rounded, character in the shape and not the surface,
   quiet tools. Works against: a stock app-icon look, mixed stroke and fill,
   texture or distress inside icons, mascots, effects, colour without
   meaning, tools louder than your own marks. Use a tiny example where one
   helps.
5. One line: "The three directions below are three ways to live by this."

The systems designer drafts the words (they own jobs.md and the ethos
synthesis). The visual designer lays the card out. I score it with the
rest. It's scored on whether a stranger could judge an icon against it
in a second.

**Two systems findings that confirm my fixes:** Timetable's sharp off star
beside the round-jointed fixed check (TS fix 1 and 2), and Field Guide and
Cut Paper tools near-identical (the cross-direction tool-grammar fix, plus
CP fix 2).

**Star-shape cost is moot as drawn.** All three keep the shipped filled
star, and only the off star (tag and add form, no gesture code) differs.
Owner Q3 drops the star clause.
