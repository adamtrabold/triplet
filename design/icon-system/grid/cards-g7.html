Batch 1, redrawn to the craft bar

You said (on batch 1)"i definitely think it's the right side for all of the icons on the first board on this page... and i definitely think the 1.5px weight feels the best right now. that said none of these are yet at the craft level i need the icons to be at -- the sizes of various parts of many of these are not yet optically pleasing, the rounding at various intersections feels wrong (some places too broad roujnding, some too tight), and some of the choices inside of the icons don't make sense (for example why does the coffee cup handle cut off on the bottom - it creates a narrow passage that will definitely disappear at small sizes...and why is the interior of the museum pillars totally rounded pill shapes...these are just examples). i also think some of these decisions are contributing to poor optical alignment inside of the badge -- some feel like they're floating above center, some feel slightly lower on center. none have the same optical weight exactly yet when viewed as a set."
And"i want them all to be focusing on best practices and principles for icon design"

The same six (café, bar, museum, nature, the + and the Star), redrawn at 1.5px. Every change below comes from an established icon-design practice, named where it's used:

  • Optical centring: each glyph is placed by its mass, halfway between its box centre and its ink centroid, not by the box or BASE.
  • Overshoot: pointed and round tops rise past a flat top (the tree's tip by 1/2px) so they look the same height.
  • Equal optical weight: checked by squinting (a blurred row) and by ink share; wide glyphs are drawn shorter.
  • Proportion: parts sized to each other: a coupe's foot about 70% of its bowl, a low classical pediment, a cup about 1.4x as wide as deep.
  • Counters that survive small sizes: no passage narrower than the line; every hole at least 1.5px (the cup's handle is a D-shaped ear, its counter 2.25 x 2px).
  • Joins and corners by angle, and by size: a round never exceeds 0.2 of the shortest edge at its corner, so slots and line ends stay square-cornered (no pills).
  • Joins add no ink: where a line meets or crosses another part, the join meets square: no round to fill it, no notch cut into it (no ink traps, your call): the + crossing, the coupe's stem, the trunk.
  • Contrast compensation: horizontals are drawn about 10% thinner than uprights (1.33px against 1.5px), because a horizontal reads heavier.
  • Pixel fitting: horizontals are 4/3px, exactly 4 device pixels on a 3x phone, and sit on whole device pixels.

Sheet 1 is the set, judged together at real size, beside two mature icon sets (Material Symbols and Phosphor). Sheet 2 is each drawing, before and after. Sheet 6 is the grid, changed by this redraw.

The grid, changed by the redraw

Your feedback says a grid of guides alone can't give optical centring or equal weight. These changes add the practices that do. Each one is shown before and after on the icons it fixes; all six were re-checked under each.

Change 9: a round fits the parts it joins

Practice: join and corner treatment by angle and by size. A round is never more than 0.2 of the shortest edge at its corner. It replaces the pill ends that the collision rule (change 7) made on narrow slots; change 7 stays as a fallback and now fires nowhere in this batch.

Batch 1: the slot ends in a semicircle (a pill)
Now: a square slot end, its corners square (change 14)

Change 10: placed by optical centre, not by the box

Practice: optical centring. The point put on the badge's centre is halfway between the drawing's box centre and its ink centroid. TOP, BASE and MIDDLE stay as drawing guides inside the box; they no longer set where the drawing sits in the badge. Dots: each drawing's optical centre.

Box centred
Optically centred

Change 11: equal optical weight, judged blurred

Practice: equal optical weight. Each batch is checked as a blurred row and by ink share (sheet 1). Wide drawings are drawn shorter (the museum is 10.3px tall) so they don't look bigger. The ink figure is a guide; the blurred row decides, and is now measured (src/blur-measure.js: the darkest fifth of the live circle after a 1.5px blur).

Change 12: horizontals 4/3px, on whole device pixels

Practices: contrast compensation (a horizontal reads heavier, so it is drawn about 10% thinner) and pixel fitting (4/3px is exactly 4 device pixels on a 3x phone). Horizontal edges sit on the 1/3px lattice and placement moves in 1/3px steps, so plinths, saucers and the + crossbar are crisp. Uprights stay 1.5px and centred, so their edges fall half a dot off: symmetry wins over snapping for uprights. Orange lines: device pixels on a 3x phone.

Batch 1: a 1.5px plinth; its top edge falls between device pixels
Now: a 4/3px plinth; both edges on device pixels

Change 14: joins meet square

Practice: a join adds as little ink as it can. Where a line meets or crosses another part at about a right angle, a round fills the corner and the join reads darker (g7, webbed). g7c thinned it with ink traps; you turned that down: "i do not want ink traps... no reason for that on digital imo". Now the join is square, with no round and no notch, under all three laws: the + crossing, the coupe's stem at bowl and foot, the trunk, the ear on the cup, the columns on the beam and plinth.
