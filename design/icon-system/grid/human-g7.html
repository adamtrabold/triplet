More human, by rule

You said: "i still find myself pining for that original shape. i think it's the fact that it feels more delicate. that more humanistic approach to curves gives it a handmade feel that i think calls back to the physicality of making vintage work." Below, three tunings of the whole set, each a grid rule applied to all of them, not a redraw of one, at true size (with the 12px chips) and at 7x. Counters and the size classes stay as they are.

The question
Which of these, if any, has the delicate, hand-made feel you're after? Or which parts of one (the flares, the drooping branches, the domed foot)?
The feeling: batch 1's coupe
Shown for the feeling only, not as a target. What made it feel drawn by hand: its stem flared into the bowl and the foot (its joins took a round of about a line), its foot was a dome, not a slab, its bowl a full curve, and its tree (not shown) had drooping branches. Those are the qualities the tunings carry, as rules, to every icon.
Now (v134): monoweight
The set as it is: every join takes the tight inside round (0.15px); the star as shipped.
H1: monoweight, plus flares at joins
Where a post meets a mass (a stem, a trunk, a handle), the join FLARES with a round of one line (1.5px) that may sweep up to 0.45 of the shorter edge, so joins sweep instead of meeting at near-points. Counters, slots and notches stay tight. The star's points reach as far as the +'s arms.
H2: monoweight, flares, a domed foot, drooping branches, and air
H1, plus three rules: a foot that holds a glass up by its stem is a DOME (a new root shape); a tier's underside lifts toward the trunk by half a line, so the branches droop; and the fit stops at 11/12 of the cut, so no glyph is pushed to the edge of its box.
H3: monoweight relaxed: free posts 1 1/4, flaring into their joins
H2, plus: a post that stands free (the coupe's stem, the trunk) thins to 1 1/4px between its joins and flares out of them with a line and a half (2.25px), as batch 1's stem did. Walls, counters and every other line stay 1.5.
Rules that bend

Interior tighter than exterior (your rule) bends at one kind of corner only: a join where a post meets a mass. A flare is an inside corner rounder than the outside ones next to it; that is what makes it read as pulled, not cut. Counters, slots and the tree's notches stay tight in every tuning.

Every base is a slab (your root-shape ask) gains one exception in H2 and H3: a foot that holds something up by a stem is a DOME. Today that is only the coupe; later it would be a lamp, a trophy, a bell. A base something sits flat on (the saucer, the plinth) stays a slab.

Monoweight (your pick) holds in H1 and H2; H3 relaxes it for free-standing posts only, and says so in its name.

The Outline class: in H1 to H3 the star's points reach as far as the +'s arms (the shipped star's 4px hole minimum made it 15% wider than v133, the biggest yet); its hole is then 2 lines, the old minimum.

The fit (H2, H3) stops at 11/12 of the cut. The + has no joins to flare.

Not used
No other icon set is used as a model here (you: Material and Phosphor are "not an example of good craft"). Apple's published method for drawing glyphs is studied for practice only; none of its glyphs are shown.
