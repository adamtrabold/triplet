The grid scales the whole drawing until its widest or its tallest part meets the cut, whichever comes first; nothing passes the cut except a lone apex, by 1/2. So a vessel's rim, a saucer or a plinth may span the cut's full width, and it is the class that sets that width, never the drawing. A drawing's own numbers (the coupe's rim is 11.5 of its 12-unit box) are proportions, not sizes. Measured on the shipped glyphs, to the 1/3px lattice.

Ink settling on a screened banner: soft, never blotted. A round is never more than 0.2 of the shorter edge at its corner. No ink traps.
Line weight
Monoweight: one line, the ring's weight, everywhere; a hole's inside runs parallel to its outside, horizontals and verticals alike. Line marks (the Outline and Open classes, made only of lines, no mass) carry 7/6 of it, 1.75px, so they come closer to the weight of the solid places without reading bold.
Stacked gables
All by rule (shown: the taller-tip option; with equal tiers the tip shows h too): the tip tier is the tree's point and shows 1.5h, the tiers under it h each; the tip's half width is the shelf plus a third of a line, the bottom tier spans the tall class (9 1/3) and the middle steps evenly between, so each tier is steeper than the one below; every notch shelf one and a half lines (2.25px), so a fir reads as a fir at 100%; the trunk shows one line; the apex passes TOP by 1/2. Inside rounds tighter than outside.
