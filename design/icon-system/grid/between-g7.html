Yes, I see it. It isn't at the corner, it's how the line changes speed as it leaves the corner. The v223 rounds met each edge without a step in how hard they bend, but not in how fast that bend changes:

The coupe's flares (bowl into stem, stem into foot): the bend switched on at full rate the instant the line left the bowl, the stem or the foot, and came to a point at the corner. The line goes from steady to turning with a jolt: a knee, just outside the join, on both sides. That is the wobble you saw "further away from the point".

The v223 check passed all three because it measured the bend itself (a step, a flat spot, a ripple), not its rate of change. A check that passes what your eye fails measured the wrong thing; the new one (below) fails all three.

The plan, as rules in the engine
Rounds drawn by their bendEvery round, tips and joins alike, is built from how hard it bends: the bend rises from the edge's own (a line's none, a bowl's gentle one) by an even ramp with no jolt, crests round with no point, and eases back the same way. It reaches exactly as deep into its corner as before, so every shape keeps its size. Nothing changes speed on the line just outside a corner, and the bend can't creep out along the edge.
A corner into a bend the other wayWhere a corner runs into a short drawn bend the other way (the museum's beam end into its capital), the two are one S, and it lands on the next edge with that edge's own bend.
The fewest curves that hold itEach half of a round is one curve where one follows the ramp, else two. Tips now take four curves where they took two.

The proof, on every outline: a new check (src/fair-check.js) walks each outline and fails a knee (a jump in how fast the line bends, over a quarter of the local crest's bend squared), a tail (a round not halfway up its bend until more than 70% of the way along it), a dip (a round that eases off and tightens again) and a step in the bend itself, lines included. It fails all three of your marks on v223. This row: v223 172 faults (the old check: 0), after 4 (the cup: 4 jump); the other row 6. The cup's are its handle (below); the other row's two more are the ends of its domed coupe foot, where the bend still runs up late (the next fix on the list).

The museum: building the flaring in

The rule: Joins (in the table above). The coupe's flaring is where its stem meets its bowl and foot. A building has the same meeting: its outer posts meet the beam they hold and the base they stand on. Joins flares them there, on the outside, as the stem does; the openings stay square. The museum in the row now takes it.

Your separated gable rests on the beam across the 1px cut; the beam is back and the columns keep their full height. The gable has its body back: its ends are 1px (they were 0.43 in v220, a sliver). Where the room came from: the class. With the gable set on its beam the temple is as tall as it is wide, so, by the rule Class follows drawn proportion, it fits the square class (the coupe's), not the wide one; that gives it the height for the gable's ends. Flag: this moves the museum from Wide to Square on your decided master grid. The pitch is kept and the columns are as tall as before.
