2 · The museum: architecture and sizing
You: "how might this style be reflected in architectural things like this that are a lot of straight lines"

The beam-shed museum is gone ("no this is awful"), and its rule with it. H2's museum is the base. Two ways to bring the set's hand to a building of straight lines, each a rule a hotel, city blocks or the curtain's frame could follow, each shown beside the cup, coupe and tree at true size. Each keeps all its parts; the weight is shown as it is. (Swelling columns and drooping lines are dropped: they pinched the openings and made the building sag.) The first changes how the museum sits in the row at true size; the second is felt at 72px and up.

H2's museum (the base)
As H2 drew it: four columns, a beam, a straight roof, a plinth. Every approach below starts from it.
Weight (blurred darkness, overall / darkest fifth): 32.5 / 69.5; the cup 28.3 / 70.6, the coupe 21.3 / 57.6, the tree 18.0 / 59.4.
Sized by its weight
A shape that fills its whole box, as a building does, is drawn a tenth smaller than its class, so its blurred weight sits with the cup's rather than above it; every part stays. The rule for any straight-lined building (a hotel, city blocks): it is sized by its weight, not its box.
Weight (blurred darkness, overall / darkest fifth): 27.2 / 71.0; the cup 28.3 / 70.6, the coupe 21.3 / 57.6, the tree 18.0 / 59.4.
Cast soft, joins flared
The building's outside corners round by 0.6px, as the cup's and coupe's do, and where a column meets the beam or the plinth the opening's corners round by half a line, as the coupe's stem flares. Lines stay straight; the hand is in the corners and joins. It combines with the first.
