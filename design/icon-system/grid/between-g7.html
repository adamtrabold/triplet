3 · The museum: a pattern for built things

Built things keep their straight lines and wear at their joins. The cup, the coupe and the tree loosen in their curves; a building has few, so it loosens where its parts meet:

  1. No heavier than the rest. A building is the heaviest shape in the row by eye, so its openings are at least two lines wide: three columns, not four. Blurred (the darkness inside the live circle after a 1.5px blur, src/museum-blur.js): the museum went from 32.3 to 29.5; the cup is 28.3, the coupe 26.1, the tree 17.9.
  2. Beam, columns and plinth stay straight. The roof's slopes sweep in a tenth of a line, half the tree's sweep, so the roof is drawn, not ruled.
  3. Where a column meets the beam or the plinth it has a capital and a base: the opening's corners round to 0.5px (under the 0.6 of an outside corner, so inside stays tighter than outside, and an opening never becomes a pill).
  4. The outer columns meet the beam and plinth with the same 0.5 round.
  5. Slab ends (beam, plinth, steps) take the line-end round, 0.3 at most, as the plate's ends do.
  6. A base on the ground stays flat (the table-or-ground rule).
