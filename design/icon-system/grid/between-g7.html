0 · The joins and the centring, as rules
You: "these are looking so so good. a few things that still stand out. vertical centering should be optical always on a per-icon basis. the join patterns for sharp corners like this tree don't seem to be the same quite yet at a pattern level - these look like slightly different approaches (screenshot attached). there also seems to be a slight wobbliness at some of the joins because of the rounding mechanics on various icons (a few screenshots attached). not sure how to solve, but they should be architectural and smooth with no visible humps or wobbles... they should feel impeccably constructed at every join."
You: "im still concerned that the counter edge looks less curved than the exterior edge... this is a screenshot of the left edge, but it's the same on the right."
Optical centring, per iconEach icon is set on the badge by its own optical centre: its box's centre, pulled down by half the distance its ink sits above it, so a top-heavy drawing (the cup, the coupe) sinks toward its weight; a drawing heavy at the foot (the museum on its plinth, the tree on its trunk) is not lifted, its clearance above and below is what the eye compares. Placed exactly, no 1/3px snap. The star and the + by the same rule in their slots.
One join patternA corner's treatment follows its kind, never the length of its edges: every outside corner of a mass (a tip, a rim, an apex) takes the same eased curve; every inside corner, flare, notch, counter and line end takes one compact round. The tree's upper tips were plain arcs and its bottom tips eased only because their edges were longer (the engine's old 3px test); now all three tiers end the same way.
No humps: every join builtEvery round meets its edges with their own curvature and bends evenly to its tightest point and back (one peak, no flat-then-round). Where a corner runs straight into a bend the other way (the museum's beam end into its capital), the two become one S. A counter's end that runs beside a curved wall is one curve, so it follows the wall (the coupe's band).

All three live in the engine, so every icon gets them, and so will the next one. No drawing changed: the coupe, the tree's shape and the museum are as they were (each the same size); only their joins, the band's ends and where they sit.

Five rules, each a value for every icon. The tree follows all of them and comes out byte-identical; the coupe changes only in its drink.

RuleWasNow
PlatesH3's dome turned under: ends thin to a pointA plate departs from flat by 1/2px at the centre, so its ends stay 1px (2/3 of the line). What a thing sits on curves underneath, flat on top.
Here: Cup: the saucer. The coupe's foot: see the question below. Museum, tree: they stand on the ground, flat (unchanged).
Posts1 1/4px for a free post; the columns exemptEvery post that holds a mass up is 1 1/4px, columns included.
Here: Museum: the four columns, so each opening is 1/3px wider (the outside edges stay). Coupe stem, trunk: already. +: holds nothing up, 1 1/2.
Resting parts(no rule; the cup was cut from its saucer by the 1px cut)A load set on separate supports is cut from them by the 1px cut: the cup on its saucer, a roof on its beam. Parts made as one piece (a glass, a tree, a colonnade with its beam and base) join. Limits: at most one cut per icon; both parts read whole on their own; the cut never shortens the supports: its room comes from the load or the class.
Here: Museum: the gable rests on the beam across the cut; beam, columns and base are one piece; the columns are full height. Cup: as it was. Coupe, tree: one piece each, unchanged.
Class follows drawn proportioneach place icon's class set by hand (cup, museum: wide; coupe: square; tree: tall)A shaped icon's class (wide, square, tall) is the one whose proportion is nearest its own drawing's, measured on its finished outline. The + and the star keep theirs.
Here: Museum: with its gable set on its beam it is as tall as it is wide, so it moves from Wide to Square on your decided master grid (flagged: your call). Cup, coupe, tree: unchanged (checked in every row). A future flat-roofed hotel comes out wide; a gabled one, square.
Joins (a post into what it holds and stands on)(no rule for buildings: only free posts flared)Where an outer post meets the mass it holds up or stands on, it flares 1/2px into it on the outside, as the coupe's stem meets its bowl and foot; past the post that mass thins to a 1px end (the plate rule). An opening is a counter: it stays square, no flare inside.
Here: Museum: the outer columns flare into the beam and the plinth (capital and base). Coupe stem, trunk: as they were. A future hotel: its corner walls flare into cornice and base, windows square; a landmark or venue on posts: the same at its outer posts.
Straight-lined buildings(no rule)A building's walls, beams and roof slopes stay straight; sweep and droop are for living things. The 1/2px a plate rule gives a base and a vessel's curved side are not touched by this.
Here: Museum: the pediment stays straight. Tree: keeps its sweep and droop.
The clear band under a rim7/6px5/6px, so the drink's surface sits on the next 1/3px lattice line up. Still under the line, as you asked ("equal to the thickness of the stroke. or maybe even a little thinner").
Here: Coupe only. 1px was tried: at 24px it didn't show.
Unchanged: line ends, tips by their edge, flares 1/2px, mass corners 0.6, the vessel side, the tree's sweep and droop. The + and the star: nothing in these rules acts on them.

The museum: building the flaring in
You: "When I say connected I mean to the systemic approach. I like the separated gable … it's the flaring effects that the museum and other structures like it aren't going to naturally make. How might we build some of that in?"

The rule: Joins (in the table above). The coupe's flaring is where its stem meets its bowl and foot. A building has the same meeting: its outer posts meet the beam they hold and the base they stand on. Joins flares them there, on the outside, as the stem does; the openings stay square. The museum in the row now takes it.

Your separated gable rests on the beam across the 1px cut; the beam is back and the columns keep their full height. The gable has its body back: its ends are 1px (they were 0.43 in v220, a sliver). Where the room came from: the class. With the gable set on its beam the temple is as tall as it is wide, so, by the rule Class follows drawn proportion, it fits the square class (the coupe's), not the wide one; that gives it the height for the gable's ends. Flag: this moves the museum from Wide to Square on your decided master grid. The pitch is kept and the columns are as tall as before.
