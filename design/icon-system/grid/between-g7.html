The plan, as rules in the engine
Rounds drawn by their bendEvery round is built from how hard it bends: the bend rises from the edge's own (a line's none, a bowl's gentle one) by an even ramp with no jolt, crests round, and eases back the same way. Joins keep their depth, so every shape keeps its size.
Sharp tips keep their pointA tip (a corner sharper than 75°) keeps v223's nose: the same tightest bend at the point. Its bend rises late (as the square of the even ramp), so the flanks run straight right up to it, and the tip lands exactly where v223's point was: the two edges are re-aimed by a hair to meet there, so the tip keeps its length and the tree its size.
Drawn bends start from no bendA drawn arc that runs on from a line with no corner (the cup's handle) ramps up from no bend over the first 3/10 of its half, then holds a slightly tighter arc to its middle, which it reaches exactly. The arc moves in by 0.15px at most at 72px.
A corner into a bend the other wayWhere a corner runs into a short drawn bend the other way (the museum's beam end into its capital), the two are one S.
At most two curves a halfEach half of a round is one curve where one holds the bend within the knee limit, else two, never more; the two follow the bend itself, not only its place, so they can't stall where they meet.

The proof, on every outline: the check (src/fair-check.js) fails a knee (a jump in how fast the line bends), a tail (a round not halfway up its bend until 70% of the way along), a dip (a round that eases off and tightens again), a shoulder (the bend stops growing partway and starts again) and a step in the bend itself, lines included. This row: v223 178 faults (4 jump, 141 knee, 27 tail, 6 shoulder; the old check found 0). The first try (v225) 22 (4 jump, 18 shoulder). Now 0, and 2 on the other row (the domed foot).

You: "im also not sure the weight is perfect yet on the plus and star... plus *might* feel a bit light (can't exactly tell because...) the star feels heavy"

Both are drawn with the same 1 2/3px line, and the same line reads at different weights. The star's five walls stand close around a small hole, so at reading size they run together into one dark shape; the +'s two bars stand alone. Blurred the way the eye sums them, at true size, the star reads 53 and the + 39.

The rule: each line mark's line is solved so it reads at one darkness, the same for every line mark, so a future line mark comes out matched. It is solved at true size (the + in its 26px slot and the star in its 20px slot, as they sit beside the 24px badges), because that is where you read them. At 72px a thin line reads lighter than it does small, so there the solved star can look spindly next to the +: compare the right half of each row. The one open value is how dark. RuleWasNow

Here: Cup: the saucer. The coupe's foot: see the question below. Museum, tree: they stand on the ground, flat (unchanged).
Posts1 1/4px for a free post; the columns exemptEvery post that holds a mass up is 1 1/4px, columns included.

Resting parts(no rule; the cup was cut from its saucer by the 1px cut)A load set on separate supports is cut from them by the 1px cut: the cup on its saucer, a roof on its beam. Parts made as one piece (a glass, a tree, a colonnade with its beam and base) join. Limits: at most one cut per icon; both parts read whole on their own; the cut never shortens the supports: its room comes from the load or the class.
Here: Museum: the gable rests on the beam across the cut; beam, columns and base are one piece; the columns are full height. Cup: as it was. Coupe, tree: one piece each, unchanged.
Class follows drawn proportioneach place icon's class set by hand (cup, museum: wide; coupe: square; tree: tall)A shaped icon's class (wide, square, tall) is the one whose proportion is nearest its own drawing's, measured on its finished outline. The + and the star keep theirs.

The rule: Joins (in the table above). The coupe's flaring is where its stem meets its bowl and foot. A building has the same meeting: its outer posts meet the beam they hold and the base they stand on. Joins flares them there, on the outside, as the stem does; the openings stay square. The museum in the row now takes it.

Your separated gable rests on the beam across the 1px cut; the beam is back and the columns keep their full height. The gable has its body back: its ends are 1px (they were 0.43 in v220, a sliver). Where the room came from: the class. With the gable set on its beam the temple is as tall as it is wide, so, by the rule Class follows drawn proportion, it fits the square class (the coupe's), not the wide one; that gives it the height for the gable's ends. Flag: this moves the museum from Wide to Square on your decided master grid. The pitch is kept and the columns are as tall as before.
