You saw it right. The foot's top was one drawn curve (already within 0.03px of a circle at 72px), but its two ends were not part of it: at each top corner the engine drew a round of its own. So the top read as a run with a knuckle at each end. The plate under the cup was built the same way, upside down.

The rule now (the cap rule): a base with a curved side is a slab cut by one ellipse, 7/6 of the base's width and its full height. The ellipse makes the whole curved side, shoulders included; the slab's ends cut it a little inside its widest point, and only those cuts are rounded (by the law, as every cut is). A stem stands on its crown (the foot: the ellipse on top); a thing sits on its flat (the plate: the ellipse underneath). It replaces the plate rule and the domed foot: one rule for both.

0b · Before this round: the hump at the coupe's flares (v228), and the star's counter
You: "This is almost perfect. On the corner types on the coupe, there is still an awkward hump on the bowl and foot on either side as a result of the round shape. I'm not sure how to smooth it further programmatically but let's do one more try. Also think the problem with the star weight is the counter size now. I think the stroke is inner stroke so it's making the counter shape feel cramped. Let's find the right balance there for the outline icons."

The hump, named: a shelf. The bowl and the foot meet the stem almost level. The round there was an even hill: its bend was split half and half between the stem and the bowl (or foot). So the bowl ran on nearly level right up to the round, making a short flat shelf, and the round sat on that shelf as a separate knuckle. That's the hump on either side.

Why the check passed it: it measured how smoothly the bend changes, and the bend did change smoothly. It never asked where the bend sits. A new check, shelf, does: at a round between a line and a gentle curve that bends the other way, the run along the curve must be at least 1 1/2 times the run along the line. v228 fails it at all four coupe flares.

The rule, in the engine (every corner of this type, in any icon): the leaned flare. A round between a line and a gentle curve that bends the other way leans onto the curve. Its run along the curve is 1 3/4 times its run along the line, and it crests at the corner law's own radius (the even hill crested tighter). So the curve starts bending into the stem earlier and there is no level stretch for a knuckle to sit on. Why 1 3/4: it is the most an even hill can lean while keeping a real run on the line side (at 2 that run shrinks to a step).

0c · Outline icons: the line and the counter

You were right: it was an inner stroke, in effect. The star was sized by its outside (its points as far as the +'s arms) and drawn with the line marks' 1 2/3px line, so every bit of weight came out of the counter: 3.1px across. The star's own class rule asks for a bigger counter, but the row's star never used it.

The rule, for the two kinds of line mark. Outline marks (the star; later a heart, a pin) are drawn like the solids: the set's 1 1/2px line (the walls of the cup and coupe), centred on the drawn shape, and sized by the counter: at least 2 1/2 lines across at the centre. The outside follows. Open marks (the +; later an x, an arrow) keep 1 2/3px: a stroke standing on its own, with nothing facing it, reads lighter. So at 72px the star's line is visibly thinner than the +'s arms; that is the rule, not a slip. The star: 13.2 → 14.1px across (the +'s arms: 13.2); its counter 3.1 → 4.0px across, with 60% more open area.
