Blind test: two new icons from the spec
You: "ideally future ones come out perfect on first try." The director's test for a 10: two new icons drawn blind from the Construction board's spec, run through the checks and put in the row. If they land first time, it's a 10. If not, the rule that failed gets fixed.

How it went. Drawn by a designer who had seen only the spec, the code it names and your taste rules, not how the six were made. The person landed first time: both checks clean, and it reads at 12, 24 and 72px. The bed did not. Two rules failed on it. Rule 11 ("placed by weight") put the pillow in the middle of the mattress. And a whole ellipse as flat as the pillow (1/2 as tall as wide) fails the fair check, though it is a pure root shape. So it's not a 10 yet: two rules to fix, and six places where the spec didn't say and I had to choose (listed below). The bed below is shown twice: as drawn blind, and with the two fixes. The second is not hand-tuned: both changes are rule changes.

The two rules that failed, and the fix for each
Rule 11, placed by weightAs written: "A part that rests on another sits with its ink centre over the other's centre." The pillow rests on the mattress, so it is centred on it: a bed with a pillow in the middle. The rule was written for the cup on its saucer, a part resting on one thing alone. Fix: "A part resting on one other part alone sits with its ink centre over that part's centre. A part that rests on one part and against another (a pillow on a mattress, against the headboard) sits against it, across the same cut."
A whole ellipse, how flatThe pillow, a pure ellipse 1/2 as tall as wide, fails the fair check: 4 TAIL faults, at its two ends (the bend creeps up and then shoots up at the ends, 0.75 of the way before it is halfway). Measured on whole ellipses at pillow size: 1/2 fails, 3/5 fails at 3.7px wide and passes at 5.5px, 2/3 and rounder always pass. The six never met this: every vessel ellipse is 4/5. Fix: a rule, "An ellipse drawn whole is at least 2/3 as tall as it is wide; anything flatter is a rectangle the law rounds, or a cap." (Or the check exempts a root ellipse's ends. I'd rather the rule: it keeps the check strict.)
Where the spec didn't say, and what I chose
1 · Outline marks with two counters"Outline (sized by its counter, 2 1/2 lines across)" assumes one counter. The person has two. I chose: the smallest counter across sets the size (the shoulders', up the axis), because the rule is there to keep holes open. Sizing by the head instead would make the person 13.5px wide and leave the shoulders' counter 2.4px, under 2 lines.
2 · An outline mark's slotThe star is drawn "in its own 24 box at 20px"; nothing says what slot a new outline mark gets. I chose 24px, the account button's glyph size in the app (#accountBtn's svg).
3 · Which corner law for outline marksRule 14 gives one table (mass corners 0.6). The code draws the star with a different law, the joint law with a tip share, scaled to its slot (mass corners 0.45). I used rule 14's, since that is what the spec says; the person's only outside corners are the shoulders' base, 0.6.
4 · One line vs the 1/3px latticeThe legs show one line (1 1/2px), but horizontal edges sit on the 1/3px lattice and 1 1/2 is not on it. I chose the first lattice line at least a line below the mattress (as the cup's saucer does with its cut), so the legs show 1 2/3px.
5 · A slab or a mass"The line: every wall, slab, beam and plinth." A mattress is a horizontal block, but at a line thick the bed reads as a bench. Nothing says when a block is a slab (a thickness) and when it is a mass (a shape). I chose a mass: 1/4 as tall as wide.
6 · One cut, two faces"A cut of 2/3 line between parts that rest on each other (at most one per icon)." The fixed pillow is 1px from the mattress and from the headboard. I counted that as one cut: one part, cut free on two faces.
Also chosen (inside the spec)Side view, not front: a front view's two pillows come out under 2px wide at 24. The headboard is a post (it holds the bed's head up), not a wall. Legs reuse the trunk's "shows one line" (the spec says reuse a value where the job is the same). The shoulders reuse the vessel ellipse, 4/5. New values are new lines in src/proportions.js (PROP.bed, PROP.person). The master grid board still says outline holes ≥ 4px and a 1 2/3px line; I followed the spec (2 1/2 lines, 1 1/2px).
