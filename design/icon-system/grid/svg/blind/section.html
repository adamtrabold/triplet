Blind test, round 1: what failed and the rules that fix it
You: "Yeah let's try for a ten also it's be best if the underlying shapes were proportionally related to each other and the grid so they weren't just random." And: "ideally future ones come out perfect on first try."

What happened. A designer who had seen only the spec drew a bed and a person. Both passed the checks, but by eye neither looked like one of the family: the person was too big (16.76 × 16.19px against the star's 14.07), and the bed was the right weight but generic, "a library bed", with none of the flares, caps or cut-free parts the six have. The director scored the system 7. The cause was the spec, not the designer: it said how to build an icon, not what makes one ours. Below are the seven gaps it found, each now a rule in the code and in the spec, and the two icons re-drawn by me under the fixed spec. That is a check, not a blind test: a fresh designer draws two other icons next.

The seven gaps, worst first, and the rule that fixes each
1 · Nothing carried the family's character into a new joinRule 4, the joins. Every place two roots meet is one of five kinds, and the kind decides how it looks: holds (a post flares a step into what it carries), overhang (a slab on two or more posts passes its outer posts by 1/3 post and drops a step, the flare and drop one curve: the capital; a mass on legs takes holds only), rests (cut free on every face it touches, flat on flat), base (one support: a cap; several posts: a slab with holds and overhang; legs: none), free (a post ends square; a living mass hangs to a tip). Each is taken from the six (figure below). The capital is now one function, joins.js heldSlab, which the museum is drawn with (byte for byte as before).
2 · Outline sizingRule 15. An outline mark is sized by its outside to the outline cut: Wide, Square and Tall scaled so Square is the star's width on screen, 14.07px. It is drawn at its slot with the line 1 1/2px on screen. Its counters are a check, at least 2 lines (3px), not the sizer. Two counters stacked at 2 1/2 lines need 14.5px of height, more than the cut, so 2 1/2 stays the star's own value, not the floor.
3 · The spec's corner table differed from the code'sRule 17: one table (PROP.rounds), with two columns: shaped marks, and line marks (outline and open, the star's law: no flares, mass corners 0.45). The engine is now built from it (laws7 fromTable), and rounds-check.js proves it gives the same answer as the laws the six were built with, corner by corner (164,880 cases). The six rebuild byte for byte.
4 · Slab or mass, undefinedRule 5. A block that carries or caps something (beam, plinth, saucer, foot) is a slab, one line. A block that is the thing (the cup's body, a mattress) is a mass, a ratio of the widest root, at least 2 lines tall. Test: if it still names the thing drawn one line thick, it's a slab.
5 · The line (1 1/2px) off the 1/3px latticeRule 10. The lattice places a part's first edge. A thickness that is a part (line, post, slab) is exact, and its far edge may sit off the lattice. A space or a shown length (a cut, a gap, how much of a post shows) is a minimum: the next part starts on the first lattice line at least that far away. That is what the cup's saucer already did; legs that show one line show 1 2/3px.
6 · "One cut per icon"Rule 14. At most one part per icon is cut free. It is cut on every face it touches, all at 1px, and that counts as one cut.
7 · The master grid was out of dateRebuilt from the code (master-board.js): the outline cut, the fit read from the engine, slab and mass, the joins, the one round table, the lattice rule. The old one is on the Archive page.
And the two the blind designer proposedRule 13, placed by weight, scoped: a part resting on one part alone sits centred on it; a part resting on one part and against another sits against it. Rule 2, a whole ellipse: at least 2/3 as tall as wide; flatter is a cap or a rounded rectangle. Both kept as he wrote them, as the director ruled.

Did the six change?

No. Their svgs are byte for byte the same: the museum is now drawn by the shared capital function and every icon's rounds come from the one table, and both give exactly what they gave before (construction.js: 6 of 6 identical).

The 1/1000 rule is now the engine's (turn 159): every new build has its offsets and positions written to 1/1000 of a unit, so a new icon's designer never meets it. The six stay as you approved them. Rebuilt under it, they move 0.0001 to 0.02px, nothing you could see, but the tree's top tip gains a TAIL in the check (and if sizes are rounded too, the coupe's rim does instead). Rounding moves the engine's knife-edge cases; it doesn't remove them. The real fix is in how the engine solves a round, and that is the next engine job.
