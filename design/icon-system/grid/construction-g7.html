The series, for shapes: the fractions of a parent root

Halves, thirds, quarters, fifths and sixths, or a whole and one of them (1 4/5, 2 1/2). The parent is another root: the widest root is 1.

Every rule, in one list
1Roots. Every piece is one root shape drawn whole: a circle, an ellipse, a rectangle (slab or post), a triangle (gable), a regular star, a straight line. Name each piece's root before drawing; an edge you can't name that way is the one to fix.
2Connect and cut. Roots are joined into one outline, or cut apart: a counter, a 1px cut between parts that rest on each other (at most one per icon), a slab's end cutting an ellipse.
3A curved edge belongs to its root, ends included. No end draws its own arc; rounds only where roots meet or are cut. An ellipse meets a straight edge parallel to its side at its widest point, tangent, never cut. A base with a curved side is a cap: a slab cut by one ellipse its full height, the slab's ends cutting it at half its height (on top under a stem, underneath under a thing that sits).
4A counter is a root. The same shape on the same centre made smaller (circle in circle, star in star), the space a spacing leaves, or a root moved (the bowl's side moved in). An ellipse counter: the same ellipse less a wall on each axis. A counter that runs into a wall runs straight for half a line first.
5One series. Every proportion is a fraction in halves, thirds, quarters, fifths or sixths, or a whole and one of them (the rulers above).
6Shapes are fractions of a root. The widest root is 1 (the class fit makes it the cut). Every other root is a fraction of it or of the root it belongs to (the table).
7Thicknesses are fractions of the line. The line is 1 1/2px and never scales; posts, cuts, gaps, steps and counters across are fractions of it (the second ruler). Outline marks keep the line; open marks (the +) draw 1 2/3px, set by darkness.
8Vessels. A vessel's curved side is one ellipse, 4/5 as tall as it is wide.
9Placed by weight. A part that rests on another sits with its ink centre over the other's centre (the cup on its saucer). A symmetric icon is drawn about its axis and mirrored.
10The class. Wide, Square or Tall: whichever cut of the coin is nearest the drawing's own width to height, read at its fitted size; a lone apex passes the cut by a step (1/2px). Line marks: Outline (sized by its counter, 2 1/2 lines across) or Open (arms span 12).
11The fit. Positions scale, thicknesses never do, until the finished outline meets the cut × 11/12 in width or height (solved, not stepped).
12Rounds, by kind. The law: mass corners 0.6, wide outside 0.7, line ends 0.3, tips 0.35 or 0.0877 of the shorter edge, joins, notches and counters 0.15, wide inside 0.3, flares 1/2px (a step); never over 0.2 of the shorter edge. Every round is a smooth hill of bend, not an arc.
13Two rules of transition. At a flare into a gentle curve bending the other way, the round runs 1 3/4 as far along the curve as along the line. A tip whose round would be wider than 1/6px goes soft (one wide hill).
14Centred by eye, by rule. Across: half-way between its box centre and its ink centre, to 1/4px. Up and down: its box centre, pulled down by half of how far its ink sits above it.
15Checked. The fair check clean (knee, tail, jump, inflect, shelf, shoulder, kink); looked at at 12, 24 and 72px and beside the other six.

The steps
Step 1
Name the roots
List each piece and its root shape (rule 1). Pick the widest root: it is 1.
Step 2
Give every root a ratio
Each root's size and place as a fraction of its parent from the series; each thickness as a fraction of the line (the table: reuse a value where the job is the same, e.g. any post is 5/6 line, any vessel 4/5). Write the ratios down before drawing.
Step 3
Draw it in the 12 box
Widest root across the box; the rest from their ratios; horizontal edges on the 1/3px lattice; caps, vessels and counters by rules 3, 4 and 8.
Step 4
Let the engine finish it
It joins and cuts, picks the class at the fitted size, fits to the cut, rounds every corner by its kind, mirrors, and centres it by rule.
Step 5
Check it
The fair check clean; at 12, 24 and 72px; in a row beside the six.
Step 6
If something won't snap
Show the nearest series value and the cost beside your value, and mark it "won't snap", as the tree's tiers are.
Step 7
Put new values in the table
A new job gets a new line in src/proportions.js (the code reads it; nothing else holds a value).
Step 8
Show it
On the board, at true size first.
The proportions of the four shaped icons, on the engine's own construction

Orange: the roots as built; dashed brown: an ellipse drawn whole; dashed orange: a cut. 1px grid of the 24 badge. The star: a regular ten-point star, inner radius 0.47, its counter the same star on the same centre, sized so it is 2 1/2 lines across. The +: two posts of the open-mark line, its arms spanning the coin.

The cup
  1. The cup is 1 (its width); it is as deep as it is wide.
  2. The saucer, the widest root, is 1 4/5 of the cup.
  3. The ear is 4/5 of the cup's depth, a half circle at its back; its counter a circle on its centre, a line in, running straight from the wall for half a line.
  4. The bottom: one ellipse wall to wall, 4/5 as tall as wide, the walls tangent at its widest point.
  5. The saucer: a cap, its ellipse underneath, the slab's ends cutting it at half its height; 1px below the cup.
  6. Balanced: the cup and ear's ink centre over the saucer's centre (dashed).
The coupe
  1. The bowl: one ellipse, the widest root (the glass's width), 4/5 as tall as wide.
  2. The rim line cuts it 1/5 of its height above its centre, so the bowl is 6/5 of the ellipse deep and its sides turn from the rim on.
  3. The stem, a post (5/6 line), shows half the bowl's depth.
  4. The foot: a cap, its ellipse 4/5 as wide as the bowl's, the ends at half its height. The band: half a line, the bowl's side moved in a line.
The museum
  1. The roof: a gable on 4/5-line ends, rising 1/3 of its run; it rests on the beam across the 1px cut.
  2. The beam, the widest root, passes the outer columns by 1/3 of a post; past them its underside drops a step, and the outer columns flare a step into it.
  3. Four columns, posts (5/6 line), 1/3 of the colonnade's width tall; the openings are the spaces they leave.
  4. The plinth: the beam's rule, upside down.
The tree
  1. Three gables. Their shown heights (4.83 : 3 : 3.33) and half widths (2.8 : 4.1 : 5.3) won't snap: v123's rhythm.
  2. A notch is 3/5 of the half width of the tier above; each tier droops half a line and sweeps in a fifth of a line.
  3. The trunk: a post (5/6 line) showing one line under the bottom tier; the apex passes the cut by a step.

The four counters, as shapes

Walls stay even wherever the hole is the outside's own shape on the same centre (circle in circle, regular star in star) or the space between parallel posts. A future counter in an ellipse takes the same ellipse less a wall on each axis (scaled instead, its walls would run thicker at the ends).

The cup's ear
A circle on the ear's own centre, a line less than the ear's radius, run on to the cup's wall as a slot half a line long. Two concentric circles: the wall is even all round, 1 1/2.
The museum's openings
Rectangles: what the four columns' spacing leaves between the beam and the plinth. The columns are posts (5/6 line), 1/3 of the colonnade's width tall.
The star's counter
The same star on the same centre, 0.53 its size, 2 1/2 lines across. A regular star's ten edges all stand the same distance from its centre, so the smaller star leaves every wall the same, 1 1/2.
The coupe's clear band
The bowl's own side (solid) moved in sideways (dashed), between the rim and the drink: a root moved, not an offset. The band is half a line tall; walls 1 1/2 across, measured square to the bowl 1.43 → 1.35 → 1.24px down the band.

What the construction shows

Drawn from the engine's own record, these are where the build doesn't reduce to clean shapes, or where one job is done more than one way. (v233's second finding, the counters, is answered: see Roots.)

  1. The curve count is the price of no wobbles. You asked for joins with "no visible humps or wobbles". A compass round can't give that: its bend switches on at once where it meets a line. So every round here is a hill of bend, 2 to 4 cubics, rising from the edge's own bend and falling back (the dashed circle is only its tightest point). That makes the set curve-heavy: café 48, bar 62, museum 92, nature 72, star 80, plus 48. For comparison, Lucide (checked in its source) draws on a 2px centreline: its cup's corners are single arcs, and its star and museum have no drawn rounds at all; the stroke's round join softens them.
  2. Some drawn curves are only aims. The museum's capital and foot curves set where an S begins and ends; the engine draws the S. The cup's loop is eased in where it leaves a straight. The tree's tiers are re-aimed so each tip lands where the eased tip was: the drawn points move about 0.4px (top tier) to 1px (bottom tier). So the orange shapes are exact everywhere except at those places.
