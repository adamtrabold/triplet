How a seventh icon is built: the engine's order
Step 1
Draw it from root shapes
In the 12-unit box: slabs (bases, beams), posts (stems, columns, trunks), gables (roofs, tiers), vessels (bowls), loops (handles), domes (plates, feet). Lines 1 1/2; a post that holds a mass up 1 1/4; horizontal edges on the 1/3px lattice; a part that rests on another is cut 1px from it.
Step 2
Let its proportion pick the class
Wide, Square or Tall: whichever cut of the coin is nearest the drawing's own width to height (a lone apex counts 1/2 more). Line marks: Outline (sized by its counter, 2 1/2 lines) or Open (arms span 12).
Step 3
Size it to the cut
Positions scale, line widths never do, until the finished outline just meets the cut × 11/12 in width or height.
Step 4
Join, then cut the counters
The shapes are united; counters and gaps are subtracted. A counter keeps the wall a line wide (see the findings: four ways today).
Step 5
Round every corner by its kind
The law sets a radius by angle and by what meets (mass corners 0.6, line ends 0.3, tips by their edge, joins and counters 0.15, flares 1/2; never over 0.2 of the shorter edge). Each round is solved as a smooth hill of bend, not an arc.
Step 6
Mirror
A symmetric glyph keeps its left half after the rounds and mirrors it, so both sides are exactly alike.
Step 7
Centre it by eye, by rule
Across: half-way between its box centre and its ink centre, to 1/4px. Up and down: its box centre, pulled down by half of how far its ink sits above it.
Step 8
Check it
The fair check (knee, tail, dip, shoulder, inflect, shelf, step), at 12, 24 and 72px by eye, and beside the other six.

What the construction shows

Drawn from the engine's own record, these are the places where the build doesn't reduce to clean shapes, or where one job is done more than one way. They're findings, not changes: no drawing changed for this board.

  1. Every round is a hill of bend, not a circle. Each is 2 to 4 cubics whose bend rises from the edge's own and falls back; the dashed circle is only its tightest point. No compass draws them; only the engine can. That is what keeps the lines leaving a join smooth, and it is also why the set has many curves: café 45, bar 60, museum 92, nature 72, star 80, plus 48. Central or Lucide would use one curve a corner.
  2. Counters are made four ways. The star's counter is its outline moved in by the line. The coupe's band is the bowl's side shifted sideways by the line. The cup's ear is two quarter curves drawn to follow its back. The museum's openings are just the gaps the posts leave. All four keep the wall a line wide, but only the first is one rule a seventh icon could follow without choosing. Question: should one of them become the rule?
  3. Some drawn curves are only aims. The museum's capital and foot curves set where an S begins and ends; the engine draws the S. The cup's loop is eased in where it leaves a straight. The tree's tiers are re-aimed so each tip lands where the eased tip was: the drawn points move about 0.4px (top tier) to 1px (bottom tier). So the orange shapes are exact everywhere except at those places.
  4. The mirror hides one seam. Built whole, the museum's right half has a 0.0004px step where the capital meets the outer post (the drawing rounds to 3 decimals, the post doesn't), so its outer opening's corners would take 0.10 instead of 0.15. The mirror replaces the right half with the left, so it never shows. An asymmetric icon wouldn't have that safety net.
  5. Every slab is the line, 1 1/2. The 4/3px horizontal in the drawing code's notes isn't used by this row: monoweight makes bases and beams 1 1/2. Only the posts that hold a mass up (1 1/4), the coupe's band (5/6) and the + (1 2/3) differ.
