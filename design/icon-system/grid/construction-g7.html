The rule, for every icon
1Every piece is one root shape, drawn whole: a circle, an ellipse, a rectangle (a slab or a post), a triangle (a gable), a regular star, or a straight line. Its size and its place are proportions of the grid and of the other roots.
2The form is roots connected and disconnected: joined into one outline, or cut apart (a counter, the 1px cut between resting parts, a slab's end cutting an ellipse).
3A curved edge belongs to its root, ends included. No end draws its own arc. The only rounds are where roots meet or are cut, and the law sets each by its kind.
4A counter is a root too: the same shape on the same centre made smaller (circle in circle, star in star), the space a spacing leaves (rectangles between posts), or a root moved (the bowl's side moved in for the band). Sized by proportion; never by offsetting the outline by the stroke.
5For a seventh icon: before drawing, name each piece's root and its proportions. An edge you can't name that way is the one to fix.

The four counters, as shapes and proportions

You asked whether it changes anything: it doesn't. Three were already root shapes and are now named as them; the star's is rebuilt as a smaller star and comes out the same. Monoweight holds, for a reason that is about shapes: the walls are even wherever the hole is the same shape as the outside on the same centre (circle in circle, regular star in star) or the space between parallel posts. Where that isn't true, proportion alone would let the walls vary: a smaller ellipse inside an ellipse, scaled, is thicker at its ends than its sides. So a future counter in an ellipse takes the same ellipse less a wall on each axis: in an 8 × 10 ellipse with a 1 1/2 wall that keeps the walls at 1.49 to 1.50 (scaled instead, they would run 1.50 to 1.88).

How a seventh icon is built: the engine's order
Step 1
Draw it from root shapes
In the 12-unit box, every piece one root drawn whole (see Roots): slabs (beams), posts (stems, columns, trunks), gables (roofs, tiers), vessels (bowls), loops (handles), caps (plates, feet: a slab cut by one ellipse). Lines 1 1/2; a post that holds a mass up 1 1/4; horizontal edges on the 1/3px lattice; a part that rests on another is cut 1px from it.
Step 2
Let its proportion pick the class
Wide, Square or Tall: whichever cut of the coin is nearest the drawing's own width to height (a lone apex counts 1/2 more). Line marks: Outline (sized by its counter, 2 1/2 lines) or Open (arms span 12).
Step 3
Size it to the cut
Positions scale, line widths never do, until the finished outline just meets the cut × 11/12 in width or height.
Step 4
Join, then cut the counters
The shapes are united; counters and gaps are subtracted. A counter is a root too: the same shape on the same centre made smaller, the space a spacing leaves, or a root moved; sized by proportion, never by offsetting.
Step 5
Round every corner by its kind
The law sets a radius by angle and by what meets (mass corners 0.6, line ends 0.3, tips by their edge, joins and counters 0.15, flares 1/2; never over 0.2 of the shorter edge). Each round is solved as a smooth hill of bend, not an arc.
Step 6
Mirror
A symmetric glyph keeps its left half after the rounds and mirrors it, so both sides are exactly alike.
Step 7
Centre it by eye, by rule
Across: half-way between its box centre and its ink centre, to 1/4px. Up and down: its box centre, pulled down by half of how far its ink sits above it.
Step 8
Check it
The fair check (knee, tail, dip, shoulder, inflect, shelf, step), at 12, 24 and 72px by eye, and beside the other six.

What the construction shows

Drawn from the engine's own record, these are where the build doesn't reduce to clean shapes, or where one job is done more than one way. (v233's second finding, the counters, is answered: see Roots.)

  1. The curve count is the price of no wobbles. You asked for joins with "no visible humps or wobbles". A compass round can't give that: its bend switches on at once where it meets a line. So every round here is a hill of bend, 2 to 4 cubics, rising from the edge's own bend and falling back (the dashed circle is only its tightest point). That makes the set curve-heavy: café 50, bar 62, museum 92, nature 72, star 80, plus 48. For comparison, Lucide (checked in its source) draws on a 2px centreline: its cup's corners are single arcs, and its star and museum have no drawn rounds at all; the stroke's round join softens them.
  2. Some drawn curves are only aims. The museum's capital and foot curves set where an S begins and ends; the engine draws the S. The cup's loop is eased in where it leaves a straight. The tree's tiers are re-aimed so each tip lands where the eased tip was: the drawn points move about 0.4px (top tier) to 1px (bottom tier). So the orange shapes are exact everywhere except at those places.
