Two grids

A grid is the board every icon is drawn on. Each sheet shows one grid as a grid:

Outside: the circle, the air, the live area, the keyshapes and the squares or rings everything snaps to.
Inside: the guides that keep the glyphs consistent with each other, with all 13 laid over them to show it working.
The chip: its own 6 × 6 grid of 2px modules; its inside guides come with the drawings.
Corners: the grid's corner rule.

You asked"I am glad we have the outer stuff defined in that grid by what about the interior? There's nothing to keep them consistent outside height/width and space to the ring"

The inside of each sheet is the answer: shared guide lines (or rings) every glyph stands on and reaches to, one set of part sizes, two gap sizes and a minimum hole. The same rules hold in both grids; each grid lays its guides out its own way.

Settled in both: every line 2.00px; a separate drawing per size; places solid. Icons on these sheets are rough skeletons placed on the grid to show it in use, not drawings.

Your brief for the build, word for wordyes they need to sit in the stroked circle but also make sense on their own. they need to be at their root simple shapes, but joined together to make coherent glyphs. they must be crafted with the highest attention to detail and quality -- minimal bezier curves with perfect architecture. personality in the approach to how strokes are connected and curve. slight rounding of interior and exterior corners to add a vintage softness. corner rounding should not be 100% consistent though as there are different architecturally sound prinicples to how different angles intersect. the effect of the rounding should allude to something screened or pressed.

The chip

At 12px the glyph stands alone, without the circle. It gets its own simpler drawing on a 6 × 6 grid of 2px modules: six lines across. Both grids hand over to this same chip grid, because at 12px a 2px line leaves no room for anything finer.

Corners

An elbow, a T-join and the conifer's notch, enlarged 9x.

By joint: joins filleted 1.1px, cut ends 0.35, tips 0.3. The curve lives where strokes connect. Dashed orange: the sharp construction.

These corners and this sheet's air (2px) are shown as an example. Corners and air go with either grid; they are a separate pick after this one.

Precedent: Material Design's keyline grid; type design's baseline, x-height and cap height (shared horizontal guides).

How we'd draw on it

We recommend drawing as code on the grid you pick. Each glyph is a list of simple shapes placed on the guides; a script joins them into one SVG path with real holes and applies the grid's corner rule, so a change to a guide or a rule reaches all 13 at once. Figma via its MCP is the alternative if you want to push curves by hand; its write tools are in beta and this environment has no Figma connection.
