Grid change 1: size by class, the token

Every glyph is fitted by the grid, never by hand, to a cut of one 12px coin with the coin's area: Wide 12 × 9 1/3, Square 10 2/3, Tall 9 1/3 × 12, Round for radial marks (the hull through the Star's tips has the coin's area), and Open for marks made only of lines (the +: its arms span the coin and every line is the full 1.5px, no thinner horizontal). A lone apex (the pediment, the tree's tip) passes the cut by 1/2px. The drawing sets proportions; the class sets size. A new icon picks its class and is fitted the same way.

Grid change 2: root shapes, like parts cut from one shape

Before, the three bases were three shapes: a tapered blade, a dome and a slab (your example). Now every base is the same slab, every bowl the same vessel, every roof and tier the same gable, every upright the same post, every handle and outline the same loop. The coupe's bowl now holds a drink under a clear band, so it reads as a glass, not a chalice.

What about the grid is ours
The token
A glyph is struck like a token: a 12px coin that sits inside our badge's ring with 2px of air, as a seal sits in a signet ring. Every glyph is cut to the coin's area, in the shape its subject needs (wide, square, tall, round, open), so none reads bigger than its neighbour: a set of pins struck from one blank.
The root shapes
Like a woodcut or a case of metal type, the set is cut from a few blocks used again and again: the slab is the bed every object stands on, the post the upright of a road sign, the gable a roof, the vessel and the loop.
The corners
Every meeting point is slightly round and the inside settles tighter than the outside: ink settling on a screened banner, not a ruled line (your words).
The 1/3px lattice
Slabs, rims and liquid lines sit on whole device pixels, so their edges print crisp, as a screened edge does, while the corners stay soft.
