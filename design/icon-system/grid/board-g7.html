One token, five root shapes

You asked for size and root shape to come from the grid, not from icon-by-icon fixes. This adds both to the grid: how big a glyph is now comes from its shape class, and parts that do the same job are cut from the same shape. Latest change (g7j): an Outline size class for the Star, sized so its hole always stays open (an outline means "not starred yet"), and a rule for stacked gables: every notch in the tree is the same depth, one and a half lines (g7k: deeper, so the tiers read at 100%). Latest (g7p): monoweight is the line everywhere; the + and the star draw their line at 1.75px (line marks); the tree has its earlier shape back by rule; the coupe's wider bowl is back with more drink; the cup's broken corner is fixed. Before is v105; now is the same six, refitted by the rules. Every icon is a live vector: zoom in on the canvas.

Grid change 1: size by class, the token

Every glyph is fitted by the grid, never by hand, to a cut of one 12px coin with the coin's area: Wide 12 × 9 1/3, Square 10 2/3, Tall 9 1/3 × 12, Outline for outline marks (the Star, whose outline means "not yet tapped": the hull through its tips at least the coin's area, and its hole at least two lines across, so it never reads as filled; at 1.5px the hole decides), and Open for marks made only of lines (the +: its arms span the coin). The Outline and Open classes are line marks: with no mass to carry weight, they draw their line at 1.75px (7/6 of the line), even all along. A lone apex (the pediment, the tree's tip) passes the cut by 1/2px. The drawing sets proportions; the class sets size. A new icon picks its class and is fitted the same way.

Grid change 2: root shapes, like parts cut from one shape

Before, the three bases were three shapes: a tapered blade, a dome and a slab (your example). Now every base is the same slab, every bowl the same vessel, every roof and tier the same gable, every upright the same post, every handle and outline the same loop. Where gables stack (the tree), the top tier is the point and shows one and a half tier heights, the widths step evenly from narrow to the tall class, and each notch shows the same 1.5-line shelf. The coupe's wide bowl holds a drink under a clear band, so it reads as a glass, not a chalice. The cup's bowl keeps both its walls; the handle is its own loop, joined to it.

What about the grid is ours
The token
A glyph is struck like a token: a 12px coin that sits inside our badge's ring with 2px of air, as a seal sits in a signet ring. Every glyph is cut to the coin's area, in the shape its subject needs (wide, square, tall, round, outline, open), so none reads bigger than its neighbour: a set of pins struck from one blank.
The root shapes
Like a woodcut or a case of metal type, the set is cut from a few blocks used again and again: the slab is the bed every object stands on, the post the upright of a road sign, the gable a roof, the vessel and the loop.
The corners
Every meeting point is slightly round and the inside settles tighter than the outside: ink settling on a screened banner, not a ruled line (your words).
The 1/3px lattice
Slabs, rims and liquid lines sit on whole device pixels, so their edges print crisp, as a screened edge does, while the corners stay soft.

Weight: the line marks

In our blur, a 1.5px line can't get darker than about 58 and the solid places reach 75, so the Star and the + read light. You found 1.5px too light and the heavier row far too heavy; the line marks now draw at 1.75px, between the two. The Weight board, to the right, shows the new row between both rows you saw.
