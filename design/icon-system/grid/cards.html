The grid, three ways

You asked for a shared grid to draw the 13 on, and more than one concept. Each grid below sets the frame, the sizes, the shapes it allows and a corner law. The 13 appear on each one as skeletons: plain shapes, so you can judge the grid, not the drawing.

Your brief for the build, word for wordyes they need to sit in the stroked circle but also make sense on their own. they need to be at their root simple shapes, but joined together to make coherent glyphs. they must be crafted with the highest attention to detail and quality -- minimal bezier curves with perfect architecture. personality in the approach to how strokes are connected and curve. slight rounding of interior and exterior corners to add a vintage softness. corner rounding should not be 100% consistent though as there are different architecturally sound prinicples to how different angles intersect. the effect of the rounding should allude to something screened or pressed.

Today every glyph is one 24-unit drawing, shrunk: a part 2 units wide is 1.5px in the badge and 1px in the chip. Your settled line is 2.00px at every size, so a grid has to say what happens at 12. On your phone 1px is 3 screen dots, so a 0.5px rounding is 1.5 dots: slight, but there.

The three
  • A · Pixel plate. One grid square = one screen pixel at the size it shows. A separate drawing for each size.
  • B · Seal. A round grid in the badge, one drawing for every size, and a pen that stays 2px.
  • C · Press kit. The 2px line is the grid square. Glyphs are built from a kit of module parts, smallest size first.

A · Pixel plate

One grid square = one screen pixel at the size it shows. A separate drawing for each size.

Construction, the 24 badge enlarged (14.9x)
live circlepoints may reachair between glyph and ringkeylines
  • Canvas: the 24px badge itself. Ring 2, then 2px of air, then a live circle Ø16. Points may reach Ø17.
  • Keylines inside the live circle: circle Ø16, square 12, upright 10×14, wide 14×10.
  • Straight edges sit on whole pixels; symmetric parts use even widths so they centre on 12.
  • Shapes: rectangles, circles and arcs, 45° diagonals; other slopes as whole-pixel steps (1:2, 2:3).
  • Every line is 2px. A solid is never thinner than 2px; a cut or gap is never less than 1px.
  • Sizes: a 24 drawing (row badge, near pin and, at 1:1, the selected pin) and a 12 drawing (chip).
  • Corners by joint: the end of a line 0.5, the outside of a mass 1.0, a tip 0.4, an obtuse shoulder 1.25, a join 0.6, a narrow notch 0.3.

Precedent: Material (24 canvas, 20 live, keyline shapes), IBM (whole-pixel coordinates), Fluent (a drawing per size).

What it costs
  • Two drawings per icon: 26 to draw and keep in step.
  • The selected pin shows the 24 drawing at 1:1, so the glyph does not grow when the pin does; the extra room is field.
  • With 45° as the only free diagonal, the walker and the wheel's stand come out angular.

Grid · card 2 · concept B

B · Seal

A round grid in the badge, one drawing for every size, and a pen that stays 2px.

Construction, the 24 badge enlarged (14.9x)
live circlepoints may reachair between glyph and ringkeylines
  • Canvas: the 24px badge. Ring 2, then 1.5px of air, then a live circle Ø17. Points may reach Ø18: the picture fills the seal, as on a coin or a stamp.
  • Keylines are polar: rings at r 3, 5.5 and 8.5 round the centre, and rays every 15°.
  • Curves share the badge's centre where they can (the coupe's bowl, the bag's handle, the wheel).
  • Two kinds of part: masses, which scale; and lines, which are centre-lines drawn with a 2.00px pen at every size.
  • Sizes: ONE drawing. The selected pin enlarges it 1.41x, the chip shrinks it to 0.71x; the pen stays 2px, so the chip gets bolder, and parts marked "detail" drop out below 0.8x.
  • Corners by angle, even wear: every outside corner loses 0.22px from its point, every inside corner fills 0.3px. Tips stay sharp, right angles soften, obtuse corners go round.

Precedent: SF Symbols (one design, interpolated; margins per weight), coin and seal engraving (the device fills the field), monoline type (a skeleton plus a pen).

What it costs
  • 13 drawings, one per icon, and the selected pin grows with them.
  • The chip is the badge drawing at 0.71x with a 2px pen: the wheel loses its spokes, the column front drops to two columns, the cup's handle closes.
  • The glyph sits 1.5px from the ring, not 2: fuller, and closer to the rim.

Grid · card 3 · concept C

C · Press kit

The 2px line is the grid square. Glyphs are built from a kit of module parts, smallest size first.

Construction, the 24 badge enlarged (14.9x)
live circlepoints may reachair between glyph and ringkeylines
  • Module = 2px, the line. The 24 badge is 12 modules: ring 1, air 1, a live circle 8 modules across.
  • The 12 chip is 6 × 6 modules and is drawn FIRST; the 24 adds parts to it, never a different idea.
  • The kit: bars and blocks in whole modules, discs, halves and quarters of whole-module radius, slopes of 1:1 and 1:2. A part may sit on the half module (1px) so it centres or keeps a 1px gap.
  • Every line is one module (2px); a solid is at least one module; a cut is at least half a module (1px).
  • Sizes: a 12 drawing and a 24 drawing (the selected pin shows the 24 at 1:1).
  • Corners by press: outside corners squeeze 0.35px round, inside corners fill 0.9px. One pair of radii; the angle changes what you see.

Precedent: Wood type and printers' rule (a kit of parts on a body), Aicher's Munich 1972 grid (orthogonals and diagonals), cartographic minimum dimensions (design for the smallest size first).

What it costs
  • Two drawings per icon, and the 12 leads: the 24 may add parts, never a different idea.
  • The coarsest grid: fewest curves (245 for the 13) and the chunkiest parts.
  • Pressed corners fill small openings: the wheel's four openings come out as round holes.

Three corner laws, same shapes

Each law is a rule, not a call per corner, and each rounded corner is one curve. Close-ups on the corners that matter. Dashed orange: the sharp construction. Numbers: radius in screen px, outside and inside. Grid: 1px, with half-pixel lines.

  • A · by joint. The radius follows what the corner is: the cut end of a line 0.5, the outside of a mass 1.0, a tip 0.4, an obtuse shoulder 1.25, a join 0.6, a narrow notch 0.3 (kept open).
  • B · even wear. Every outside corner loses the same 0.22px from its point; every inside corner fills 0.3px. So a 30° tip stays sharp (0.15), a right angle softens (0.53), the 110° apex goes round (1.0).
  • C · press. Ink pressed onto paper: outside corners squeeze 0.35px, inside corners fill 0.9px. The radii don't change; what you see does: the tip blunts more than the right angle, and the notch fills.
  • Inside corners fill in all three, because that is what print does: ink spreads into inside corners first. Bell Centennial (Matthew Carter, 1975–78) cut ink traps into its joins so phone-book ink could fill them; letterpress printers call the edge build-up "ink squeeze". We draw the filled result.
  • At real size on your phone (3 dots per px), a 0.5px radius is 1.5 dots: you feel these more than see them, which fits "slight". Where they show is in the joins and tips of a whole glyph.

What the builds measured
  • Curves per glyph (24 badge, rounded corners included): A 264, B 285, C 245 across the 13. Each rounded corner is one curve; circles are four.
  • The chip. A and C draw it separately, and at 12px they draw the same thing: with a 2px line, any grid becomes 6 × 6 modules there. B shrinks its one drawing and keeps the pen at 2px, so its chip is bolder; parts marked as detail drop out, so the wheel loses its spokes and the column front keeps two columns.
  • Three picks can't keep a 2px line at 12px in A and C: the ⌘ (it doesn't fit), the wheel's spokes and the walker's arms are 1.5px there. That's a question for the drawing step, not a grid choice.
  • The ⌘'s heavier line. The team's condition for the ⌘ is a line clearly heavier than 2.00px. A 3px ⌘ with 1px openings needs a circle Ø18.3; the badge's live area is Ø16 (A, C) or Ø17 (B). It has room only at the 32 selected pin. At 12px even a 2px ⌘ with 1px openings needs Ø13.5.
Grid · card 6 · how we draw

The tool

You asked whether another tool or an MCP would be better. Our recommendation: keep drawing as code, the way these boards were made.

  • The grid is the code. Each glyph is a list of simple shapes on the grid. A script joins them into one path with real holes, applies the corner law, and writes the SVG. Change a radius once and all 13 follow; every change is a reviewable line in git.
  • It works the way your brief reads. "simple shapes, but joined together": the source is the shapes, and the script joins them. "minimal bezier curves": each rounded corner is one curve, and we count them. "should not be 100% consistent": the corner law, written once, says how each kind of corner rounds.
  • No fake cut-outs. Earlier sketches faked holes with paper-coloured shapes, which break on the navy selected pin. These are single paths with real holes (see the selected pins above).
  • Figma (via its MCP) is the other route, if you want to push curves by hand. Its write tools are in beta and this environment has no Figma connection, so we'd need you to connect it. We'd still keep the corner law in code.
