# Shopping concepts: how each works

Product designer (systems), 2026-10-26, after jam turn 3. One file per concept: `B1-blade-sign.md`, `J-sign-hangs-the-bag.md`, `K-the-fold.md`, and `L-open-glyph.md` (proposed kill). Jobs and flows F1-F5: `../jobs.md`. Grid rules used throughout (the parallel pixel round, `../../grid/pixel-fit.md`): line 1½, slabs 2, counters ≥ 2, gaps ≥ 1, protrusion ≥ 1, plus the rack ⅛ / ¼ / ½ (`../../grid/signatures.md`). Coordinates are on the 24 grid, drawn 1:1.

The ink figures are my hand estimates (net area, holes subtracted, px²), so the director can see the weight problem before the drawings exist. The visual designer's script measures the real ones.

## One finding across all three: the support is the line, the subject is the mass

The six already do this, though nobody has written it down. The coupe's stem, the tree's trunk and the museum's columns are posts at the line. The bowl, the tiers and the roof are masses. B1, J and K are built the same way: the bracket and the links at the line, the board or the bag as the mass. Proposed as a written rule: **what carries is drawn at the line; what is carried is a mass.** Every later icon then knows which of its parts may be thin.

## What the counter rule decides before anyone draws

- **Two ring links need their centres 6px apart** (Ø 5 each, plus the 1px gap). On an 8-wide board they collide. So B1 gets either one ring or a board 11 or more wide (see B1).
- **Nothing can hang *through* a 2px hole.** A ring's 1½ wall inside a 2px counter leaves ¼px slivers, under every minimum. J's "hung by its own die-cut hole" can't be drawn at 24. J keeps the idea in a form that can (see J).

## The weight problem, plainly

The director's bar is net ink at or below the café's. A solid board or bag big enough to read at 18 is 40-60px² on its own, and a bracket adds 20-25. So B1 and J land near the museum (74½) unless something gives; each file names what. K, a bag alone, is the only one that reaches the café's band without losing a part.

## Order I'd draw them in

K and B1 first (they are the two clean tests of "a bag" against "a sign"), then J (the fusion, worth it only if one of those reads half right).
