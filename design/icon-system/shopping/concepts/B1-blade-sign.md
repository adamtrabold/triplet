# B1: the blade sign (tidied)

**What it is:** the old-town trade sign, a board hung from a bracket off a wall. It says "a shop is here", which is what a pin says. It's left of centre by subject: nobody's Shopping icon is the sign over the door. It's plain by its source: it is a sign.

**Tidy (director, turn 3):** no wall plate, no chequer, one L bracket, no brace (B3 read as gallows), links as rings so it stops rhyming with the museum's columns.

## Roots and construction (24 grid)

| Part | Root | Size, place | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wall stub | post at the line | 1½ × 5: x 4½..6, y 7½..12½ | the support is the line |
| Arm | post at the line | 1½ tall: y 7½..9, from the stub to the board's far edge | joined; the inside corner is the ½ fillet (a post into a face) |
| Link(s) | ring | counter 2, line 1½, so Ø 5; centre on y 10: counter y 9..11 (the arm's underside is its top), wall to 12½ | a link is a ring, partly hidden by what it holds |
| Board | slab (mass) | see the two versions; y 12..17, corners ½ | the subject is the mass |

The ring's top wall runs into the arm and its bottom ½px hides behind the board. What shows is one clean 2px hole between the arm and the board, a "hole a hand goes through", so turn 2's rule (a handle is its counter) and the ring agree. Overall height y 7½..17, ink centre about y 12¼.

**Two versions, forced by the counter rule** (two Ø 5 rings need centres 6 apart):
- **B1a, one ring, centred.** Board 8 × 5 (x 9..17), the ring on its centre line, the arm x 4½..17. Ink about **62**. Risk: it reads as a pendant, or a padlock hung on a rail (ring above block).
- **B1b, two rings, wide board.** Board 11 × 4 (x 7..18; blade signs often are wide and low), ring centres x 9½ and 15½, the arm x 4½..18. Ink about **82** (board 44, arm 20, stub 5, two visible ring walls about 13). It's the truer sign, but it's 13½ wide (the six run about 10-12) and the heaviest in the row.

I lean B1b for reading and B1a for weight. Draw both; the blind read decides.

## Ink

The café's band isn't reached by either version. B1a sits between the coupe and the museum; B1b is above the museum. **Open for the director:** is the bar "at or below the café", or "not the heaviest in the row"? B1b can lose 11 with a 3-tall board, but 3 is close to reading as a bar rather than a board.

## The rule it adds

- **A link is a ring.** Anything that hangs, is held or is carried meets its support through a ring (2px counter, 1½ line), partly hidden. This takes the handle rule beyond cups and bags, to hangers, keys, lanterns and tags.
- **What carries is the line; what is carried is the mass** (`README.md`).

## How it looks and feels (visual, jam turn 5)

Drawn on `../sketches/contact-sheet-t5.png`. Source `bracket()`, `link()`, `sign_one()`, `sign_two()` and `sign_low()` in `../sketches/shapes.py`. The bracket is one L of line (stub x 4½..6, arm 1½ with its underside on whole px, the ½ fillet inside). Links are rings with a 2px counter on whole px. Net ink is measured:

| | Build | Net ink | vs café / museum |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1a | one ring (x 13), board 8 × 5 | 74.2 | 131% / 118% |
| **B1b: propose kill** | two rings (x 9, 15), board 12 × 4 | 94.2 | 167% / 150% |
| B1c (B1a lightened) | one ring, board 8 × 4 | 66.2 | 117% / 105% |

- **B1b: kill.** At 24 it reads as a cassette tape or a bus, because two round holes over a wide block is a cassette. It is also the heaviest mark in any row, and 13½ wide (the six run about 10-12).
- **B1a and B1c lose what made B1 work in turn 2.** The tidy-up was right in principle (no chequer, no brace), but the ring is the trouble. A round 2px hole centred over a block reads as an **eye or a lens**: at 24, a camera on a crane, or a robot's head. Turn 2's B1, with its two plain drops, read as a sign first. So at this size, ring links and the blade sign work against each other. The rule "a link is a ring" is sound for a handle, where the hole is what the hand uses, but here the hole is seen and never used.
- **Feel:** of all the drawings, the bracket gives the strongest sense of place (it stands off a wall, and you look up at it). It is also the busiest, and in a street of shops it dominates.
- **Proposal:** keep B1 alive only as B1c, for the blind read, and expect it to fail on the eye read. If the director wants the sign without J, the honest fix is turn 2's drops back in place of the ring. That breaks the museum-rhyme fix, and the director should weigh it.
