# K: the folded paper bag (F4, tidied)

**What it is:** the record or design shop's block-bottom paper bag, with a die-cut handle and the one detail a paper bag has that a carrier bag doesn't: the crease where it folds flat. F4's gusset Vs are gone; they read as ears or a crown (director, turn 3).

**Left of centre, in one line (the director's bar for any bag):** *the handle is a hole the die punched and the crease is the fold it was made with: the paper bag from a small shop, not a carrier bag.* If the director doesn't think the owner would take that line, K goes to him as the question, not as a concept.

## Roots and construction (24 grid)

| Part | Root | Size, place | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body | flared slab (mass) | top 7, foot 9 (7 : 9, J's bag), 10 tall: x 8½..15½ at the top, 7½..16½ at the foot, y 7..17; foot corners ½, top corners ¼ (a slab's own) | the subject is the mass |
| Handle | counter, a stadium | 3 × 2, centred, under a 2px band: y 9..11 | a handle is its counter |
| Crease | cut (the gap) | 1px across the full width at y 14..15, leaving a 2px slab below (the block bottom) | a fold is a cut |

- Every part is a root or a root operation: one slab, one counter, one cut. Nothing is added.
- The body's half-pixel sides are slanted edges, not landings; its top and foot, the handle and the crease all land on whole pixels.
- Odd widths (7, 9) centre on x 12 with ½px sides, as the museum's columns do after the ¼px rule. If the parallel round says even, use 8 / 10 and accept turn 2's 71.

## Ink (estimate)

Body 80, less the handle 5 and the crease 8½: **about 66**. That's the lightest of the three and within reach of the café. It's the only concept that meets the director's weight bar without giving anything up.

## The rule it adds

- **A fold is a cut.** A crease, a perforation, a hinge, a page edge: wherever material folds or tears, the system shows it as the 1px gap. Later icons that need it: a folded map (Neighbourhood), a ticket's tear line, a book's spine.

## How it looks and feels (visual, jam turn 5)

Drawn three ways on `../sketches/contact-sheet-t5.png` (2x; also `-1x`, `-3x`). Source `fold_bag()` and `flared_bag()` in `../sketches/shapes.py`. Net ink is measured (holes subtracted), against the café (56.5) and the museum (62.9):

| | Build | Net ink | vs café / museum |
|---|---|---|---|
| K (spec) | 7:9, 10 tall, slot 3 × 2, crease y 14..15 | 66.2 | 117% / 105% |
| K2 (even) | 8:10, slot 4 × 2: every level and upright edge whole | 73.2 | 130% / 116% |
| **K3 (lightened, my pick)** | 6:8, 9 tall (y 8..17), slot 3 × 2 under the 2px band (y 10..12), crease y 14..15 | **50.2** | **89% / 80%** |

- **K3 is the version to read.** It is the only drawing in the round that sits inside the six's band (34-63) at the light end, where the commonest pin belongs. It is about the coupe's height and narrower than the café, so it doesn't crowd its ring. K and K2 are a size too big: they fill the badge like a block, and the extra mass is what makes them read as a printer or a toaster at 24.
- **What it looks like at size (my read, seen, not blind):** at 72 a small paper shop bag, plainly. At 24 a bag, though the crease can read as a plinth, so it could be a flowerpot or a bin. At 18 in the ring the slot holds (1½px), and the crease (¾px) goes grey but still reads as the bag's foot. At 12 it is a small trapezoid with a dot: still not any other chip. Reversed, the slot reads before the crease.
- **Feel:** quiet and straight, like a stamped price label. Nothing sticks out, so in a shopping street it sits back where the bracket concepts don't. That is the point of it.
- **What I'd still change:** the crease tilts the read toward a stand or a plinth. If the blind read says "pot" or "bin", move the crease up to y 13..14 (a 3px block bottom) before dropping it; a bag without it is F3, which read as a kettlebell.
- **Risk:** it is a bag, plainly. It passes "plain" and "made by a tool" (one slab, one counter, one cut), and its left-of-centre claim rests on the die-cut handle and the fold, which are subtle at 24.
