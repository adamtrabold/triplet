# J: the sign that hangs the bag

**What it is:** the director's fusion (turn 3). A Nordic trade sign hangs the thing itself, cut from plate: the boot, the key, the pretzel. Here the plate is a bag. The bracket says "shop"; the shape says "goods". It's left of centre the coupe's way: everyone draws a bag, and we draw the sign a shop hangs. It must be drawn straight: no tilt, no swing, no motion lines, one fusion only.

## What the counter rule changes

The director's version hangs the bag *by its own die-cut hole*. That can't be drawn at 24: a hook's 1½ wall inside a 2px hole leaves ¼px slivers, and a hole big enough (5½ tall) is a third of the icon. A single ring centred above a holed bag is worse, because ring over hole is a shackle over a keyhole: a padlock.

So in J the bag hangs from **two short drops at its top corners** (the old trade-sign way, chains), and its hole stays clean, as the handle. The idea survives: what's hung is the goods' shape, and its handle is visible.

Why not rings for the drops, as in B1: two rings need centres 6 apart, and the bag's top is 7. The drops do rhyme with the museum's columns, but under a bag rather than a roof, so I'd accept it. That's for the visual designer to judge by eye.

## Ink (estimate)

Bag about 64, less the handle 5 and the corners, so about 58. Arm 18, stub 5, drops 3: **about 84**, the heaviest of the three. What can give: the bag at 6 / 8 × 7 (−12), so about 72, the museum's weight. The stub can't go: without it, J is a bag on a rail.

## The rule it adds

- **A hole is held, never threaded, at 24.** A counter can be a handle or a fixing point, but nothing is drawn passing through one. That follows from counters ≥ 2 and the 1½ line, and it saves every later icon (keys on rings, tags on strings) a failed drawing.
- With B1's "a link is a ring", this gives one sentence for holding: **things are held through a counter; the counter is the root, and the ring or the body is its wall.**
