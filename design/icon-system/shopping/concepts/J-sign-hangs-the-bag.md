# J: the sign that hangs the bag

**What it is:** the director's fusion (turn 3). A Nordic trade sign hangs the thing itself, cut from plate: the boot, the key, the pretzel. Here the plate is a bag. The bracket says "shop"; the shape says "goods". It's left of centre the coupe's way: everyone draws a bag, and we draw the sign a shop hangs. It must be drawn straight: no tilt, no swing, no motion lines, one fusion only.

## What the counter rule changes

The director's version hangs the bag *by its own die-cut hole*. That can't be drawn at 24: a hook's 1½ wall inside a 2px hole leaves ¼px slivers, and a hole big enough (5½ tall) is a third of the icon. A single ring centred above a holed bag is worse, because ring over hole is a shackle over a keyhole: a padlock.

So in J the bag hangs from **two short drops at its top corners** (the old trade-sign way, chains), and its hole stays clean, as the handle. The idea survives: what's hung is the goods' shape, and its handle is visible.

| Bag | flared slab (mass) | top 7, foot 9 (7 : 9), 8 tall: x 9..16 at the top, 8..17 at the foot, y 9..17; foot corners ½, top corners ¼ | the subject is the mass |
| Handle | counter, a stadium | 3 × 2 (about ⅖ of the top; the director said about ⅓, and 2 is the floor), centred, under a 2px band: y 11..13 | a handle is its counter |

Why not rings for the drops, as in B1: two rings need centres 6 apart, and the bag's top is 7. The drops do rhyme with the museum's columns, but under a bag rather than a roof, so I'd accept it. That's for the visual designer to judge by eye.

## Ink (estimate)

Bag about 64, less the handle 5 and the corners, so about 58. Arm 18, stub 5, drops 3: **about 84**, the heaviest of the three. What can give: the bag at 6 / 8 × 7 (−12), so about 72, the museum's weight. The stub can't go: without it, J is a bag on a rail.

## The rule it adds

- **A hole is held, never threaded, at 24.** A counter can be a handle or a fixing point, but nothing is drawn passing through one. That follows from counters ≥ 2 and the 1½ line, and it saves every later icon (keys on rings, tags on strings) a failed drawing.
- With B1's "a link is a ring", this gives one sentence for holding: **things are held through a counter; the counter is the root, and the ring or the body is its wall.**

## How it looks and feels (visual, jam turn 5)

Drawn on `../sketches/contact-sheet-t5.png`. Source `hung_bag()` in `../sketches/shapes.py`. The bracket is B1's L (arm y 6½..8), the drops are posts at the line with 1px showing, and the bag is the K body with its slot clean. Net ink is measured:

| **J2 (lightened, to read)** | bag 6:8 × 7 (y 10..17), slot 3 × 2 under its 2px band, drops 2px showing | 72.2 | 128% / 115% |

- **To my eye (seen, not blind) this is the strongest "shop" read in the round.** At 24 it is a bag hanging from a sign: the bracket says a place, the bag says goods. With J2's longer drops the bag hangs rather than sits on the arm, and that gap is what sells it. In J the drops are 1px and the bag looks bolted to the arm, like a T over a bin.
- **The drops are the right call over rings.** Under a bag they don't rhyme with the museum's columns, because nothing roof-like sits on them. Rings here would bring back B1's eye read.
- **Weight is the cost.** Even J2 is the museum's weight plus 15%. The bracket alone is about 22px², and it can't go: without it, J is K on a rail. To come down further, the bag would have to drop to about 5:7, and then it loses the handle's 2px band. So J is the meaning pick and K3 the weight pick, and I don't see one drawing that is both.
- **At 18 in the ring and at 12:** the drops close up and it becomes a Γ with a block, still unlike any other chip. Reversed, the slot holds.
- **Feel:** the most characterful, and the closest to the inspo's signage, yet drawn straight: no tilt, no swing. In a street of six shops it would be the loudest thing on the map, and that is the risk, given that Shopping is the commonest pin.
