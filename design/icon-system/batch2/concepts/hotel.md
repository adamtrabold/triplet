# Hotel: concepts

Job (`../jobs.md`): **getting home**: one Hotel among many pins, found first, by shape. Park owns the slab on legs (director turn 3), so the bed loses its legs. Declarations: `decl.js` (`ht-nofoot`, `ht-board`).

## A. The bed, its foot taken away (`ht-nofoot`)

**Declaration:** pillow: a block 1/3 of the mattress wide, half as deep as it is wide, at its left end; mattress: a slab the box's full width, 2 lines thick, resting on the pillow a cut apart; head: a post at the mattress's left end, a line over the top, into it. Wide class.

**Roots and rules:** block, slab, end post; the cut join; fillet into a face. No new rule.

**How it passes the jobs:** F2 (getting home): an L (the headboard and the mattress) with the pillow in its corner is a silhouette nothing else in the set has; asymmetric, so it stands out among symmetric glyphs. It no longer stands on legs, so it can't be the picnic table (F-family "one cue, one icon").

**Risks:** with no foot it can read as a chair or a lounger (the headboard as a back). The pillow is what holds "bed".

## B. The bed with a foot board, no legs (`ht-board`)

**Declaration:** A, plus foot: a post at the mattress's right end, no lower than it (h 0), into it.

**How it differs:** the foot board closes the bed at both ends, so the mattress is held between two uprights, the hotel bed's frame; it can't be a lounger. It adds a part (weight).

**Risks:** a sofa (two arms); a U.

## Which, and why (systems)

A first (lighter, it is the 9 with one part taken away, as the draw asked), B as its rival if A reads as a lounger. Both are the bed: the owner said "bed is the best here", and nothing in the jam beat it on the getting-home job.
