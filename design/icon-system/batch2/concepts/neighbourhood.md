# Neighbourhood: concepts

Job (`../jobs.md`): never on the map; in list rows (18 in the 24 diamond) and bare at 12 on chips. "This row is an area to wander, and not a Street." Elevation, not plan (director turn 3: every icon is drawn as you'd see it standing there). Declarations: `decl.js` (`nb-kvarter`, `nb-roofs`, `nb-windows`).

## A. The kvarter (`nb-kvarter`)

**What it is:** three houses of different pitch and height on one base: a few strokes for "town", made uneven (the glazing mark's unevenness) so the count of three is the difference from Museum's one gable.

**Declaration:** three columns **side by side** (widths 3 : 4 : 3, a cut apart, standing on one line), each a house: a gable (pitches 1, 2/3, 6/5) over a block (as deep as 1/2, 2/3, 1/3 of its width); base: a slab the box's full width, resting on the houses a cut apart. Diamond class.

**Roots and rules:** gable, block, slab. **Roots side by side** (new, 11-02: any kind, each column its own spine in its share of the width, a cut apart, bottoms level so a root under the group rests on all of them). The apex rule on three crests.

**How it passes the jobs:** F5: three roofs side by side is "several buildings", which a single house (home) and Museum's single gable are not. F6 (12, no diamond): three points on one base survive bare.

**First build (18, seen by me):** three houses read, but they are small against a full-width base a cut below them, which reads as a separate bar: the base wants to be the houses' own width or to go.

**Risks:** a skyline (city), which is close enough; a bar chart if the roofs are lost at 12.

## B. The roofs are the badge (`nb-roofs`)

**What it is:** the diamond's top edges are 45 degrees, so a 45-degree gable (pitch 1) follows the badge itself: the houses become the diamond's own shape.

**Declaration:** two columns side by side, each a house: a gable of pitch 1 over a block a third as deep as it is wide. Diamond class.

**How it differs:** only two roofs, both at the badge's pitch, so the ink sits parallel to the diamond's sides. It only works in the diamond; at 12 bare it is two roofs, which must still read (F6).

**First build (18, seen by me):** the clearest of the three: two houses at once, wide in the diamond.

**Risks:** a home icon (two houses may read as one with a chimney); the diamond fit (by reach) may leave it small, since its feet are wide.

## C. The lit windows (`nb-windows`)

**What it is:** "people live here": a facade with a 2 by 2 of windows.

**Declaration:** roof: a slab the box's full width; facade: a block the box's full width, as deep as it is wide, resting on the roof a cut apart, **four holes in a 2 by 2 grid, walls of one line round and between them** (the hole rule's grid: the solid left is one even lattice). Diamond class.

**Roots and rules:** slab, block; **a hole in a mass** (new, 11-02). At 12 the holes are about 1.5px and stay open.

**How it differs:** a texture, not a building; the director's "draw it to measure".

**First build (18, seen by me):** four small holes in a block under a slab: a die or a calculator. Killed by me, as expected; it measured the rule (walls a line wide leave holes under 1px apart at 18).

**Risks:** an office, a calendar, a grid view (the same waffle that sank the city blocks, now in elevation). Expect to kill; it measures how far the hole rule goes.

## Which, and why (systems)

A: it is the plainest "part of town", the count does the work, and it pairs with Street's lamps in elevation.
