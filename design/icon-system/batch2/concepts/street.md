# Street: concepts

Job (`../jobs.md`): never on the map; rows and chips. "This row is a stretch to walk along, not a Neighbourhood." Elevation (director turn 3). The person goes to Street only if the walker survives. Declarations: `decl.js` (`street`, `st-lamp`, `st-lamps`).

## A. The walker (control, `street`)

The owner's "walker is the best here" (10-15). **Declaration:** head: a disc 1/4 of the box; body: a post leaning 6 degrees, 1/4 of the height, resting on the head a cut apart; legs: two posts from the body's foot, in stride, 26 degrees ahead and 38 behind, to the bottom. Diamond class. 7 in one-shot 2 ("lighter than the set, no arms"), since helped by "parts of a figure". **Risks:** walking directions in every maps app; an "i".

## B. The street lamp (`st-lamp`)

**Declaration:** hood: a gable over the lantern, as wide, pitch 1/2, ends 1/2 of a line; lantern: a block 1/3 of the box wide, as deep as it is wide, resting on the hood a cut apart; post: a post into it, to the bottom. Diamond class. **Roots and rules:** gable, block, post; the cut; the fillet; the apex. No new rule.

**How it passes the jobs:** a lamp post is street furniture: "along a street" without a person. Tall and narrow, so in the diamond it is the opposite silhouette to Neighbourhood's wide roofs (F5: the pair told apart by width alone).

**Risks:** a lighthouse, a bird feeder, a pin.

## C. Three lamps, receding (`st-lamps`)

**What it is:** the director's "ghost echoes": a street's length read by repetition, each lamp smaller.

**Declaration:** three columns **side by side** (widths 4 : 3 : 2, a cut apart, bottoms level), each a lamp: a hood (a gable 5/3 of its lantern wide, pitch 1/2, ends 1/2 of a line), a lantern (a block 3/5 of its column wide, as deep as it is wide, a cut apart), a post showing 3 of its lantern's depth. Since every part is a share of its column, the narrower columns make smaller lamps and shorter posts: the recession comes from the widths alone. Diamond class.

**Roots and rules:** gable, block, post; **roots side by side** (new, 11-02).

**How it passes the jobs:** "a line of the same thing, going away" is a street; it pairs with A of Neighbourhood (both side by side, both elevation: three houses inside a place, three lamps along a line).

**Risks:** noise (three hoods, three lanterns, three posts at 18); a bar chart; a fence. At 12 the smallest lamp's lantern may close (counter rule) and read as a post.

## Which, and why (systems)

B: the clearest single object for "street", and it keeps the person off the set. C if B reads as a lighthouse. A stays as the owner's control.
