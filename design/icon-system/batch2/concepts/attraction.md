# Attraction: concepts

Job (`../jobs.md`): **going in to do things**, usually with a ticket: rides, castles, pools, tours. Must not be a gear (ring and spokes), must not take the drapes (Venue owns them, director turn 3). Declarations: `decl.js` (`at-bunting`, `at-wheel4`, `attraction`).

## A. Bunting (`at-bunting`)

**What it is:** a line of three pennants between two poles: the day out, flown at every fair, castle festival, pool opening. It draws the occasion, not an object, so it covers a ride, a castle and a pool alike.

**Declaration:** line: a slab the box's full width; flags: **three gables hung point down, side by side** (widths 3 : 4 : 3, the group 3/4 of the width, a cut apart), pitch 2.4, so the middle one hangs longest (the inconsistency principle, from the size alone); poles: a post at each end of the line, 5/6 of its width below it. Square class.

**Roots and rules:** slab, gables, posts. **Roots side by side** (new, 11-02: any kind in columns, each its share of the width, a cut apart, their tops level here because they hang from one line) and **a gable face down** (new, 11-02: the gable hung point down from the face above, as a cap faces up or down). End posts as the bed's head is built.

**How it passes the jobs:** F1: three points down is a silhouette no other icon has (every other point in the set points up). No ring, so no gear. F6 (12): three flags at 12 sit about a pixel apart; if they merge to a fringe, the count drops to two by the row rule's spirit (a later rule if needed).

**Risks:** a party or birthday icon. The poles are what make it a fairground gate, not a party; they may be the part to cut if it's heavy.

**First build (18, seen by me):** the pennants fuse into the line and read as a **valance or a curtain's pelmet between two posts**, which is Venue's cue (one cue, one icon). It needs the pennants to read as separate flags: hung a cut below the line (a cut apart, each held by its corner), or fewer and longer. A finding for the visual turn, not a kill.

## B. The wheel, standing (`at-wheel4`; control `attraction`)

**What it is:** the Ferris wheel, kept as the control (7-8 on execution in the one-shots), with 4 spokes and longer legs so it stands on the ground.

**Declaration:** rim: a ring the box's full width; spokes: 4 posts through its centre, upright and level (turn 0), counted as a row; legs: two posts from the rim's centre, 24 degrees off upright each way, 1/4 of its height past it. The control (`attraction`) is the one-shot's 3 spokes, turned half a step, 30 degrees, 1/6.

**Roots and rules:** ring, spokes, splay. Spokes count as a row; openings between radiating parts (each opening holds the counter minimum as built); posts from a ring's centre start in the rim's band.

**How it passes the jobs:** "ride" at a glance. F6 (12): the spokes rule drops a count rather than fill in; the 12 is the test that killed it before (a blob).

**First build (18, seen by me):** builds now (13 min under a heavy machine load, where it hung for over 30 before). Reads as a wheel on a stand; the spokes keep their openings at 18.

**Risks:** a gear, a steering wheel, a compass (with 4 upright spokes the + inside a circle is close to a crosshair). The legs are what say "a ride"; 1/4 past is the longest yet.

**System finding:** the 4-spoke build "hanging" in turn 2 was not a loop; it was the corner fairing's last-resort deep search run again on every one of the fit's rebuilds. The engine now solves each half of a round once per process (output-neutral). See `../jam.md` turn 4.

## C. The turret flag (described, not built yet)

**What it is:** a tower top with merlons and a swallow-tail pennant on a short pole: castles and fairs both fly one.

**How it would be built, with what exists now:** merlons = **roots side by side, `apart: 'comb'`** (new: the spaces between columns as wide as the columns, the comb's one width) on a block (the tower); the pennant a block at the pole's top. No "row standing on a face" rule is needed: side by side covers it. Not built this round because the pennant must carry the read (the director's note), and a pennant beside a pole is an asymmetric part the layout places only by end posts today; I'd rather see A first.

**Risks:** the "report" flag; a chess rook.

## Which, and why (systems)

A is the strongest new kind: the only downward points in the set, no gear, an occasion rather than an object. B is the control.
