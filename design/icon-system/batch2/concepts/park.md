# Park: concepts

Job (`../jobs.md`): "what if im with my kid and i just want to see what parks i marked": the chip at 12, then the map. Hanging out outside, in town. Must not be Nature's tree. Park owns the slab on legs (director turn 3). Declarations: `decl.js` (`pk-picnic`, `pk-swing`).

## A. The picnic table, end on (`pk-picnic`)

**What it is:** the US park sign is the picnic table (road-sign pictograms).

**Declaration:** top: a slab 3/5 of the seat's width; legs1: a row of 2 posts, 1/3 of the row's span tall, held under it (chamfer); seat: a slab the box's full width, held; legs2: a row of 2 posts, 1/4 of the row's span tall, held. Wide class.

**Roots and rules:** slab, row, slab, row; the chamfer under an overhang; rows. No new rule (built from the bench's rules: the bench proves the system, the table uses it).

**How it passes the jobs:** F3: a narrow top over a wide seat is a stepped silhouette nothing else has; the bed has no legs now, so slab-on-legs is Park's alone.

**Risks:** end on, the true picnic table's legs are an A; these are upright, so it may read as a two-tier stand or a side table.

## B. The swing (`pk-swing`)

**What it is:** the owner's own "with my kid".

**Declaration:** bar: a slab the box's full width; frame: **two posts from the bar's two ends, 12 degrees out from upright, to the bottom**; ropes: a row of 2 posts spanning 2/5 of the bar, half the bar's span long, held; seat: a slab 2/5 of the bar's width, held under the ropes (chamfer). Square class.

**Roots and rules:** slab, splay, row, slab. **A splay from either end** (new, 11-02: the splay's posts run from the two ends of the root they name, leaning out; each starts inside the root, its outer side meeting the root's end on its underside, and ends square to itself on the bottom line); **a row spans a share of its root** (new, 11-02: posts hung from a bar, the room between them the row's span, so a row can be narrower than what it hangs from). The seat's chamfer is the existing rule.

**How it passes the jobs:** F3: a frame standing wide with something hung in the middle reads "playground" at a glance; at 12 the ropes thin by the row rule before the seat goes.

**First build (18, seen by me):** it reads as a stool or a small table: the seat is too big and the ropes as heavy as the legs. The ropes want the thinnest part (they are a row: the row rule may thin them), the seat narrower, the legs splayed further.

**Risks:** narrows Park to playgrounds (a cemetery park isn't one); a gallows if the seat is lost at 12; a goal or a table if the legs splay too little.

## Which, and why (systems)

A is the plainer sign and the US-sign precedent; B is the owner's own job. Both go forward.


## How it looks (visual, 2026-11-03; sheet `../sketches/contact-2.png`, rows `../sketches/rows-2.png`)
- **A. Picnic table: survives, strong.** It reads at once at 18 and 24, and as a table at 12. A narrow top over wider seats is a silhouette no other icon has. Park owns slab-on-legs.
- **B. Swing: kill.** I gave it a smaller seat and longer ropes (seat and ropes 1/4 of the bar, ropes 2/3 tall; declaration only), and it's still a stool or a bar stool. The system holds the seat under the ropes like a slab under posts, so seat and ropes fuse into one T. The swing would need its seat to hang free (a cut under the ropes), which would be a new relation for one concept. Not worth it beside the picnic table.
