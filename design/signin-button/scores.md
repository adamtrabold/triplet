### A, the arc badge: **9**
Based on: sheet A all sections; `shots/top-cream-390`, `top-dark-390`,
`top-cream-320`, `in-*`, `out-*`; flow sheet frames 1, 5.
The owner's own idea, executed so it carries the app's story: blank paper
until you're someone. Quieter than `+` on both maps; one object, one
footprint, both states; the strike and dry-up are the app's existing
vocabulary (STAR_POP, DRY), and the navy overprint swallowing the printed
arc is whimsy that carries meaning. One drawing hand: the person matches
the caps' stem. I would defend this to the owner as is. The only real risk
is objection 1, and the fallback is ready.

### B, the hotel-label banner: **7**
Based on: sheet B all sections; flow sheet (shared).
The most legible thing we made, and the banner notch keeps it from being
a web pill. It can't get to 9, because its weakness is the concept, not
the polish: the signed-out and signed-in states are two different objects
in two different footprints, so the state system the owner keeps asking
to match ("why are the active states not matching come on we should have
clear systems") breaks at exactly the moment it should feel most like one
thing. The two-object motion is the symptom (more frames, and the dry-up's
last speck landing on the word). Fixing it means making it round, which
means it's A.
**Don't replace it, don't show it.** Its job was to test whether A's arc
costs too much legibility. On the real-size renders it doesn't. If the
owner rejects the arc *idea* (not the size), B is the ready alternative.
The owner message says so in one line.
