# CD review: Hanging Tag v3 (round 5)

Fresh CD, 2026-10-06. I did not author, suggest or previously score any of
this work. Concept round: I kill on the idea, never the mock. Mock flaws
are fix notes.

## 1. Owner taste first (ledger rules that apply)

- The stub is a claim check, not a Visited-only area (owner's v3 note:
  Directions centred, or a segmented area with multiple buttons).
- Type not in the header band. Type is tier 2 or the bottom of tier 1;
  notes are tier 1.
- The address sits close to the name. Long notes show in full.
- The tag pops out of the pin onto a straight string.
- Whimsy that carries meaning, not added decoration. No typewriter face.
- No heavy rules or boxes. No dots. No second text row. Reduce information
  when marking state.
- No star mark unless starred (row rule). The popup still needs a star
  control, so a labelled outline control is allowed. An empty mark that
  reads as a state is not.
- Icons share one drawing language and one visual size. Effects are
  restrained. No hard dark edges where a shadow belongs.
- Signed out: actions visible but disabled. Directions stays live.
- Reuse existing controls and marks: the VISITED stamp, the star, "Directions".

## The references, and what "the good version" means

All five photos share one grammar. There is one huge destination word, a
few small caps-labelled fields in hairline-ruled cells, a perforated
tear-off stub with notches, and a reinforced eyelet on a string. The
fields on a real tag are blanks for an agent's handwriting. The stub is
the piece the passenger keeps.

The good version for us is:
- **Name = destination.** Both variants do this well. The condensed name is
  the loudest thing on the tag.
- **Stub = the thing you take with you.** Directions is that thing, so a
  centred Directions stub (claim) is the truest reading. A segmented stub
  of equal cells (seg) is also on-reference: see the Air Midwest and
  Hawaiian stubs in `4.webp` and `2.jpg`.
- **Fields hold data, not buttons.** Claim borrows the ruled form cells and
  then fills two of them with controls. A ruled form reads as "fill this
  in", so the controls start to look like empty blanks. That is a costume,
  not the grammar.
- The eyelet patch (paper tone, neutral grommet) is a quiet, correct lift
  from the Southern Pacific checks (`5.webp`). The decision to skip serial
  numbers is right.

## 2. Scores

### Variant 1: Segmented stub (`seg-*`). Rank 1. KEEP.

- **Concept: 8.** It answers "a segmented area with multiple buttons"
  directly. It keeps the owner's earlier hunch ("some sort of unique thing
  we could do on the rip off area if visited is in there") as the
  conductor's punch: a meaningful bit of whimsy that is tied to the tag
  form. Type has left the header and sits on one quiet labelled line under
  the note (`seg-typical`, `seg-busiest`), which matches tier 2. There is
  one row of controls in fixed slots, and nothing moves when toggled
  (`seg-v1` → `seg-v3`). The weakness: Directions is one of three equals,
  not the hero the owner hinted at.
- **Execution: 6 (capped).** The punched check in `seg-v2-crop@3x`,
  `seg-v3-crop@3x` and `seg-busiest-crop@3x` renders as a grey bevelled
  chrome tick with a drop shadow. It doesn't read as a hole with map
  showing through. It's a 3D effect with hard edges in a flat ink-and-paper
  icon set, which matches the rejected "harsh / hard dark edge" and "same
  drawing language" patterns. At 1x (`seg-busiest-phone@1x`) it reads as a
  grey emoji check. The chad in `seg-v2` reads as an L-shaped scrap, not a
  check-shaped chad.

### Variant 2: Claim check (`claim-*`). Rank 2. KEEP the stub, KILL the field grid.

- **Concept: 6 (capped).** The centred Directions claim check is the best
  single idea in this round. It is literally what the owner suggested, and
  it gives the one action you take with you its proper weight
  (`claim-typical-crop@3x`). The field grid above it is the problem:
  - **Boxes.** It adds a ruled table: one top rule, two vertical rules,
    three cells. That matches the rejected "heavy lines/rules/boxes"
    pattern.
  - **Doubled words.** It pairs a label with a value that says the same
    thing: STARRED / ★ YES, and VISITED / [VISITED stamp]
    (`claim-busiest-crop@3x`). That reads "VISITED" twice.
  - **Empty-state mark.** The unvisited state is an empty ☐ checkbox
    (`claim-typical`, `claim-bare`). It reads as an empty-state mark, which
    is the "no star unless starred" principle broken for visited.
  - **Mixed cells.** One cell is data (TYPE) and two are controls, all in
    the same style, so you can't tell which cells you can tap.
  - **Weight.** In `claim-bare-crop@3x` (Perlan, no notes) the grid takes
    about a third of the tag. A bare place reads as a form.
- **Execution: 6.**
  - In `claim-approx-phone@1x`, "DISTRICT · APPROX." crowds its cell rule
    at 1x.
  - The stamp overflows its cell during the drop (`claim-v2`).
  - There's a stray magenta caption fragment rendered at the foot of
    `claim-v2-crop@3x`.

## 4. Noise count, busiest view (`*-busiest-phone@1x`)

**Seg**
- Marks: about 14. Pin, string, eyelet patch, grommet, ×, name, address,
  note, TYPE+value, PLAN+value, 3 icons, 3 labels.
- Lines: 4. String, perforation, 2 dashed dividers.
- Boxes: 4. 3 segments + the patch.
- Colours: 6. Ink, grey, paper patch, orange, navy, the bevel grey of the
  check.
- Rejected-pattern match: **the bevelled check** (hard edge / harsh effect
  / off-language icon). That caps execution at 6.
- The dashed dividers are light and on-reference, so they are not counted
  as heavy.
- Three label colours (orange, ink, navy): orange marks the live action
  and navy marks a state, so the colour communicates. Acceptable.

**Claim**
- Marks: about 15. Pin, string, eyelet patch, grommet, ×, name, address,
  note, 3 field labels, 3 field values (one is the double-ring stamp),
  compass, DIRECTIONS.
- Lines: 5 + notches. String, top rule, 2 cell rules, perforation.
- Boxes: 5. 3 cells, stub, patch.
- Colours: 5. Ink, grey, tan rule, navy stamp, orange.
- Rejected-pattern matches, each capping at 6:
  - **ruled cell grid** (boxes/rules)
  - **label + value that repeat each other** (two text rows saying one thing)
  - **empty ☐ as an unvisited mark** (an empty state shown as a mark)

## 5. Hierarchy

**Seg.** Name > note > address ≈ stub labels > type line. That's correct,
except the type value is bold ink caps at the same weight as the stub
labels.

**Claim.** Name > note > DIRECTIONS > field values (ATTRACTION, ★ YES,
stamp) > address. The field values outrank the address, and the stamp
competes with the name at 1x. Wrong for tier 2.

## Fixes (numbered)

**Seg (rank 1)**
1. Redraw the punched check as a flat hole. It should be a true cut-out
   showing the map: no bevel, no drop shadow, at most a 1px soft inner
   edge. Draw it at the same visual size and stroke language as the star
   and compass. Make the chad match the hole's shape.
2. Match icon visual sizes across the three cells, measured by ink, not
   box. The compass reads small and the check reads large in
   `seg-busiest-crop@3x`.
3. Unvisited "MARK VISITED" uses a bare ring, which reads as an empty
   radio or dot. Use a light outline check, matching the outline star, so
   the off state is a labelled control, not an empty mark.
4. Give Directions a little more presence without breaking the equal
   cells: for example, the only coloured cell, or a slightly larger glyph.
   Designer's call. Show it so the owner can compare it with claim's
   centred stub.
5. Drop the type value to the tier-2 weight (ink-2 or regular, not bold
   ink caps) so it sits under the address.

**Claim (rank 2), if it goes to the owner**
6. Keep the centred Directions stub as the claim's headline idea.
7. Remove the ruled field grid. Show Star and Visited as controls that
   read as controls, not form cells, with no label that repeats the value.
   No "STARRED / YES" and no "VISITED / VISITED". Put type back on one
   quiet line, as seg does.
8. Replace the empty ☐ with a labelled control. An empty box is an empty
   state drawn as a mark.
9. Re-render `claim-approx` with a long type value and `claim-bare` with
   no notes. Neither may read as a form. Delete the stray caption from
   `claim-v2`.

**Both**
10. Add one more busiest still: starred + visited + plan stop + a long
    approximate note + signed out. That is the worst case for the stub
    and the field line.
11. Before the owner sees it, put seg and claim side by side at 1x on one
    phone-readable sheet, busiest state first.
