# CD review: popup round 4, Card File (Stand-Up / Rolodex / Dealt Out)

2026-10-05. Fresh CD. I did not author, suggest or score any of this work. I
read `docs/cd-brief.md`, `docs/owner-taste.md`, the README, the round-3 file
stills, the inspo and every round-4 still. I read no `*review*` file. This is a
concept round, so I kill on the idea, not on the mock.

## Ledger rules that apply

- "the typewriter font is unnecessary": gone in all three, and Archivo is used
  throughout. Held.
- "long notes should just show full length - the flip over is meh": full notes
  in all three (`*/approx`, 8 lines). Held.
- "the address shouldn't be closer to the place name": now one grey line under
  the name. Held.
- "some sort of unique thing we could do on the rip off area if visited is in
  there": answered by the Rolodex notch and the Dealt Out ink. Stand-Up reuses
  the stamp, which is consistent but not new.
- "Type is actually tier 2 imo — or bottom of tier 1. Notes are tier 1":
  type moved to the index tab. Held, and it's the right job for a tab.
- "No star unless it's been starred", "visible but disabled" when signed out
  (`*/signedout`), "Directions is fine". Held.
- "I like whimsy and these all seem kinda average", "so much information all
  visual hierarchy is starting to struggle", "Dots add an insane amount of
  visual noise", "two rows of text it reduces clarity", "No heavy rules or
  boxes", "Visited vs pin are not differentiated nearly enough", "Continuity
  between surfaces", "Bonus points … pin conceptually aligned".

## The two questions

**Does the owner now "see" the card file?** Only in Rolodex. In `rolo/busiest`
and `rolo/approx`, the card stands between the edges of the cards before and
after it, and that is the picture of a card drawer. Nothing else in the round
reads as clearly. In Stand-Up the file reads as an expanded row with a tab on
top (`up/busiest`, `up/typical`). Dealt Out shows the file only through the
empty gap (`out/busiest`), and the card on the map reads as a map callout. The
"file before any tap" (`*/f1`) is too faint in all three: at 1x it is the
shipped list with a 6px inset. The owner would not see a tray.

**Exciting rather than average?** Not yet. The content hierarchy is now right
and the stills are calm, but most of the whimsy lives in motion that the stills
can't carry (tip forward, deal out, punch, ink flood). As stills, Rolodex is
"good card file" and Dealt Out is the only one with a bold moment (the inked
card). Stand-Up is average.

## Ranking and verdicts

1. **Rolodex: KEEP.** Concept 7, execution 6 (capped).
2. **Dealt Out: KEEP as a donor, not as a direction.** Concept 6, execution 6
   (capped).
3. **Stand-Up: KILL.** Concept 5, execution 6.

**Best one, Rolodex: show after these fixes.** The fixes are small and mostly
subtractive, and the owner asked to see this. Fix it in one pass and then show
it with a phone-readable filmstrip.

## 1. Rolodex (`rolo/`): KEEP, concept 7 / execution 6

**Evidence.** In `rolo/busiest` the card sits in the drawer, with stop 1's
edge above and stop 3's edge below, and the header stays live. That is the
only frame in the round where the metaphor does a job: it shows where you are
in a plan. `rolo/approx` holds an 8-line note and still leaves the map down to
y≈385. `rolo/f2` (tipping forward) is the most file-like motion in the round.
Hierarchy at 1x: name > note > the foot > address > tab > edges. That is the
correct order.

**Noise count (`rolo/busiest`, the drawer only): about 22 marks.** The header
(chevron, title, 3 icons) · edge 1 (number, name) · the tab · 3 notches · card
(number, seal, name, address + ›, note, ⌄, Directions, Star, ✓ Visited) ·
edges 3 and 4 (number, two names). It has 5 horizontal edges: the header rule
and 4 card edges. It uses 3 paper tones, navy, ink, grey and rust.

**Rejected-pattern matches (each caps at 6):**
- **The notch column reads as dots.** At 1x each notch is a grey half-disc,
  and the edges repeat them in a column (`rolo/busiest-crop@3x`, Brauð and
  Café Loki). That is the "dots add noise" pattern. On the card itself
  (`rolo/v2-crop@3x`) the falling chad reads as a smudge, not a punch.
- **The edges are extra rows of text.** There are up to 4 strips of 11px
  truncated names ("Brauð & Co, the cinnamon bak…"). That is close to "two rows
  of text", and they aren't even tappable.
- **Visited is inconsistent.** Rows use the stamp, but the card uses a bare
  ✓ VISITED with a notch nobody can read. The README itself says the notch
  "would fail 'visited must be clearly different'".

**Owner objections (in the owner's voice, at 1x):**
1. "What are those grey half-circles? It looks like a bite out of the card."
2. "Why are there tiny squished names above and below? That's more noise, and
   I can't tap them."
3. "Visited is just a checkmark here, but it's a stamp everywhere else. Come
   on, we have a system."

**Fixes (numbered):**
1. On the card, visited uses the shipped row stamp (`.row-stamp`) in the foot
   slot, not a bare ✓. This is the state-system and continuity rule. It is the
   one good idea Stand-Up had.
2. Keep exactly one notch: on the open card's top edge, cut clearly (a real
   punched hole with the tray paper showing through, not a grey half-disc).
   Remove the notches from the edges. If the punch can't read at 1x without
   becoming a dot, drop it and let the stamp carry visited. Don't ship two
   visited marks that compete.
3. Edges: at most 1 above and 1 below. In Plans they show only the stop number
   and the name, and outside Plans they are plain card tops with no text. Make
   them taller and tappable (a tap opens that card), or drop the text.
   Truncated text you can't tap is the worst of both.
4. Remove the duplicate stop number. The lead shows "2" and the tab says
   "STOP 2 OF 3". The edges already show the order, so the tab carries type
   only.
5. The right-hand tab sits directly under the header's locate icon
   (`rolo/v2-crop@3x`, "ATTRACTION") and reads as a header control. Give it
   clear space from the header, or move it so it doesn't share the icon
   column.
6. Make `f1` read as a file at 1x: the tray and the card tops have to be
   visible before any tap, not a 6px inset. Use soft shadows only, and no
   hairlines (that is what the round-3 cut was about).
7. Show the owner a single phone-readable filmstrip (f1 → f2 → busiest → v1 →
   v2). The tip forward and the punch are where the whimsy is, and separate
   stills undersell it.
8. Space the foot to the spacing scale. "STARRED" and "VISITED" sit at
   different gaps from their icons (`rolo/busiest-crop@3x`).

## 2. Dealt Out (`out/`): KEEP as a donor, concept 6 / execution 6

**Evidence.** `out/busiest` and `out/typical`: the tab pointing up at the pin
is the round's best pin link ("Bonus points … pin conceptually aligned").
`out/v2`: the ink flood is the only Visited moment that would make the owner
smile. But the card on the map reads as a styled popup, and the file appears
only as the empty gap.

**Noise count (`out/busiest`): about 26 marks.** It has a dark slab of about
360×240, the gap, and the rows below with 2 stamps, star, × and +.

**Rejected-pattern matches:**
- **The dark card is a heavy box.** A navy slab is the loudest thing on screen
  for a place you're done with. It inverts "visited recedes": the rows file
  visited places quietly, while this card gets louder. It also drops the stamp
  for a bare ✓, which is the inconsistent-states pattern.
- **The empty gap shows an empty state as a mark.** The recessed box with a
  lone grey "2" (`out/busiest`, `out/typical`) reads as a broken row.

**Owner objections:**
1. "That dark block is huge. Why is the done place the loudest thing?"
2. "This is just a popup on the map again. Where's the card file?"
3. "What's the empty grey row in my list?"

**Fixes, if it were carried forward:** (1) ink less than the whole card
(the tab, or the foot); (2) the stamp in the foot, not a bare ✓; (3) a gap
that reads as a slot, not an empty row.

## 3. Stand-Up (`up/`): KILL, concept 5 / execution 6

**Why kill (the idea, not the mock).** "A row is the top of a tall card,
pulled up" looks, as a still, exactly like an expanded row or bottom sheet
with a tab stuck on top (`up/busiest`, `up/typical`). The owner said "can't
really see it", and this variant still doesn't let him see it. It also covers
the header, so sort, filter and locate are gone while a card is up. When the
card can't scroll to the first slot, it stands over the rows behind it, and
the tab lands on Þrír Frakkar's text (`up/shape`). That isn't fixable by
execution: it is what the idea does near the end of a list.

**Noise count (`up/busiest`): about 24 marks.** The 72px double-ring stamp is
the heaviest mark in the foot, and it outranks Directions.

**Keep from it:** the stamp as the card's Visited control (Rolodex fix 1), and
full-address-on-tap growing upward with the foot fixed (`up/addr`).

**Owner objections:** 1. "That's just the row expanded." 2. "Where did my
header go?" 3. "The tab is sitting on top of another row" (`up/shape`).

## Carry between Card File and Hanging Tag

- **File → Tag: the punch.** The owner asked for "some sort of unique thing we
  could do on the rip off area if visited is in there." The McBee notch is the
  paper-native answer: punching the tag's stub (a ticket-punch hole) on
  visited. That is a reduction ("coloured in with reduction of information"),
  not an added mark, and on a tag stub it would read far better than on an
  18px edge. This is the strongest idea to carry across.
- **File → Tag: the content stack.** Address under the name, the full note,
  type as a small tab or label off the reading block, and no star unless it
  has been starred. All of it holds up here and should be the shared baseline
  whichever direction wins.
- **Dealt Out → Tag: the ink.** Visited = the tag (or just its stub) takes the
  pin sticker's dark ink with a cream check. That is the pin link the tag's
  string already starts. Keep it to the stub, not the whole tag, or it is
  Dealt Out's slab again.
- **Tag → File: the pin link.** The file has no link to the pin except Dealt
  Out's tab. If Rolodex wins, consider the drawer's tab echoing the pin's seal
  rather than pointing at it.
- **How to weigh them:** Rolodex earns its place where the tag can't, in Plans
  order context and in a live header. The tag has the stronger single-object
  whimsy and pin link. If the owner wants one object on the map, it's the tag.
  If he wants the list to become the thing, it's Rolodex.
