**Hidden rules a person would actually need** (the file says the
plumbing is "never needed to predict anything"; that's not true):

1. **Town memory beats distance.** "A place's city is inferred from its
   town through the memory", so once a town is assigned, every later
   place in it goes there regardless of reach. With large rural
   municipalities this decides real outcomes (break A). Also unstated:
   whether rule 1 writes the memory on a town's first place (it must,
   for "remembers" to mean anything).
2. **A third town fix, "X is in Y too"**, appears only under "What
   exists" and T2. Rule 3 lists only "goes with" and "is its own", and
   doesn't say whether "goes with" removes the town from its old city.
3. **Which trip after a split** ("the one added to most recently"; "a
   new city joins the trip of the nearest city on its continent"). After
   any split, rule 2 ("your current trip on its continent") no longer
   predicts the trip; this plumbing does.
4. Lesser: the **anchor rename** (a city's name changes as towns
   overtake each other; reach is measured from a moving anchor), and
   **reach is a range** (60-80 km), so a place at 70 km is unpredictable
   by design.

**Contradictions / ambiguities**

1. A city is "a named set of real towns in **one trip**", but the town
   memory is **global** ("in every trip"). What does "Lund goes with
   Copenhagen" mean in a trip that has no Copenhagen city? Unstated.
2. "Merging keeps the name of the trip with more places" can drop a
   custom name held by the smaller trip, against C9's "a custom name
   survives". Concept 3 handles it (only between two named trips);
   concept 1 doesn't.
3. "Plan: an ordered list of stops **in a trip**", yet a split moves a
   city's unvisited places to another trip: a plan crossing that city
   now spans two trips. Unstated (concept 3 states "both").
4. The examples' trip names ("Scandinavia 2026", "Copenhagen 2028") are
   never generated by the model; they need a manual rename.

**Breaks (cases the jobs don't cover)**

- **A. The Golden Circle.** Þingvellir (~45 km), Geysir (~100 km) and
  Gullfoss (~115 km) are all in Bláskógabyggð municipality, which OSM
  often gives as the town for rural Iceland. Log Þingvellir first: the
  memory files Bláskógabyggð under Reykjavík, so Geysir and Gullfoss go
  to Reykjavík despite rule 1. Log Geysir first: a city named
  "Bláskógabyggð", and Þingvellir follows it. Order-dependent, and not
  predictable from the three rules.
- **B. Adding outside the current view.** Viewing Reykjavík, the owner
  logs Griffith Observatory (or Tivoli). Only C2 says the view jumps to
  the new city; for every later add it's unstated whether the view
  follows or the new place silently lands somewhere not on screen. Same
  gap in concepts 2 and 3.
- **C. A multi-city repeat trip.** Paris, Versailles and Lyon logged in
  2027 all join Scandinavia 2026 (one trip per continent). "A city
  splits off" works per city: it takes 1 split plus either 2 more splits
  and 2 merges, or splitting Paris before logging the rest (order
  matters). Job 10's "one fix" only holds for a one-city repeat.
